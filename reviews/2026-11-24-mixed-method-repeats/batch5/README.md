# Batch 5 brightening review

The batch recovered the published heartbeat in **14 Pegasi (HD 207650, TIC 287857379)** in SAP light curves. The other nine targets show flares, short-timescale variability or trends associated with gaps and pointing changes. The tests find no additional coherent brightening at the supplied Gaia orbital periods.

## 14 Pegasi

A fit to smoothed SAP pulse maxima gives **5.3046858321 d** and a maximum-light epoch of **1712.14069136 BTJD**. The median half-prominence width is **0.57458 d**. The maxima recur across S15/C1 and S56, S82 and S83/C2. Sixteen gap-screened peaks were used in the fit and have timing residuals under 0.008 d. An early-only fit using S15/S56 predicts the clean S82/S83 peaks within 0.010 d. The fits capture a repeatable optical maximum rather than an orbital periastron time.

Fourteen predicted pulses have adequate sampling in the core and both wings. All show SAP brightenings of 6.03–6.66 ppt using the median over the core, with smoothed peak heights near 10 ppt. QLP correction reduces corresponding median core excesses to within 0.28 ppt of zero. Six additional pulse opportunities are partly sampled. Several maxima are recovered only when plotting quality-excluded points. Keeping those points visible makes the effect of quality flags clear while preserving the quality selection for the timing and depth measurements. Thirteen adequately sampled half-period opportunities away from the primary pulses are flat to within 0.21 ppt. Background and aperture-centroid traces show no repeatable pulse-shaped excursion, including when the star is on different cameras.

Kołaczek-Szymański et al. identified the same ~10 ppt heartbeat in S15 and reported weak evidence of tidally excited oscillations. The present 5.30468583 d photometric period agrees to 0.00003583 d (3.1 seconds) with Petrie's 5.30465 ± 0.00003 d spectroscopic period as reproduced in their Table 5. The historical periastron clock, HJD 2429117.474 ± 0.013, projects near the S15 brightening (approximate difference 0.024 d over 5,579 cycles before heliocentric/barycentric and time-scale adjustment). Their S15-only tidal-model fit in Table 3 gives 5.30824 ± 0.00021 d and periastron 1712.0897 ± 0.0007 BTJD, about 0.051 d before the present peak epoch. These are different observables. The photometric period is closer to the established spectroscopic value. [Kołaczek-Szymański et al. 2021, Sect. 3.2.7 and Fig. 12](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202039553) · [Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.11559#S3.T3) · [Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.11559#A1.T5)

The selected Gaia NSS row has a 41.22927 d period and eccentricity 0.858. That ephemeris fails to describe the heartbeat, including a well-sampled S56 periastron center with a SAP excess below 0.5 ppt. The relationship between the Gaia solution and the published 5.30465 d spectroscopic binary needs separate investigation.

## Reproduction and files

From the workspace root, run `python -B sky/mixed-repeats-2026-11-24/batch5/review_brightenings.py` with NumPy, SciPy, Astropy and Matplotlib. The script shares the read, window and periodogram helpers in the adjacent batch 2 review. It reads sampled QLP FITS, the staged Gaia NSS catalog, archived scan measurements, and the sector/camera-specific cadence screen.

- `event-evidence.csv` records selected events, both local flux depths, coverages, and background and centroid checks. The original target queue's `best_window` often points to a dip despite the brightening label, so the figures include the exact queued window and all flagged brightenings.
- `predicted-windows.csv` records tests at the archive anchor plus the Gaia period, the catalog periastron plus the Gaia period, and selected photometric aliases. Exposure times enter as BTJD/TDB. Gaia nominal periastra are converted from TCB relative to J2016.0. The reported timing scale is a quadrature estimate without covariance. Mean anomalies and pulse times are not assumed to coincide.
- `287857379-pulses.csv` records pulse heights, widths and both all-data and early-only timing residuals. The photometric peak detector uses quality-screened SAP and Gaussian smoothing, with height ≥5 ppt and prominence ≥4 ppt. A central-gap screen determines which peaks enter the fit. The measured width uses half prominence and the common test window uses the median of fitted widths.
- Figures show SAP and corrected flux in ppt on the left axis and locally rescaled background, x and y on the unlabeled right axis. Ancillary diagnostics give the median core versus wing shift in wing MAD units. Quality-selected flux points exclude the camera-aware flagged exposure list. Raw depth is defined as deficit, so brightening yields negative depth. A covered window has ≥65% cadence coverage in each wing and core, with core spacing ≤max(0.025 d, 0.15 times the tested width).
