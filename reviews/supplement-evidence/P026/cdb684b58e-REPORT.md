# Exploratory search-gap pilot, 2026-10-31

The useful result is two strong fresh appointment candidates, TIC 382042754 and TIC 389128842, plus a known appointment-method recovery, TIC 155303130. Two additional band objects, TIC 167178300 and TIC 325573958, show real repeated eclipses but retain close-neighbour source ambiguity. None is added to the fixed 40 denominator or a paper count here. RESULTS.csv has five bounded physical assessments; independent helper review is in helper/AUDIT.md. Supervisor admission remains separate.

The fresh pilot used the namesake method: Gaia P, periastron epoch, eccentricity and argument of periastron predict conjunction windows before inspecting photometry. The source was the locally saved Gaia 20–60 day NSS population, G 11.5–12, excluding all Gaia IDs in the previous appointments target cache. Selection was by increasing G. The original 48 science sources had no remaining Gaia-eclipsing controls eligible in this untouched stratum; the attempted request for two such controls selected zero. A known recovery was identified afterwards by catalogue matching, never silently substituted for a prospectively selected control.

The first 48 contained one strong system, already in IJspeert et al. (2024). The weak broad dips in TIC 267620340 and TIC 307803633 did not establish eclipses; this was not used to refute them. We then extended to the next fixed 48 in a sealed 144-object brightness prefix. Photometry for the final 48 was never downloaded or examined. Cumulative executed selection is therefore **96 science sources**, **90 exact Gaia-ID TIC mappings**, **89 with QLP**, **90 execution rows including the no-data case**, **256 selected sector files**, with **zero failed downloads**. Six unmatched identities and one no-QLP target remain in the selected denominator. At most three latest released QLP sectors per object were tested; this is a bounded pilot, not an all-sector census. Some masks leave no valid random null windows, explicitly recorded as unavailable null statistics rather than high significance.

The two fresh prospects have four measured A eclipses each. TIC 382042754 has P 25.8926723860 days and TIC 389128842 P 22.6663428344 days. Both repeat in raw SAP, appear in an alternative TESScut aperture extraction, have deficits centred within 1.23 and 0.54 arcsec of the respective target, and have no distinct Gaia neighbour within 60 arcsec bright enough under the conservative approximate total-flux bound. Their Gaia periods and conjunction times are consistent. Stellar radii, mass functions and illustrative central durations admit the measured eclipse durations. These checks do not exclude unresolved companions. The two pilot period errors are OLS scatter standard errors, not calibrated total uncertainties or coverage intervals.

TIC 155303130 has four measured B eclipses at P 20.5247770157 days and a deeper A eclipse. It is an exact IJspeert2024 TIC/Gaia match: a useful known EB recovery, zero new-system addition. Its target-centred deficit is spatially separated from a marginally capable neighbour 32.4 arcsec away. The five reports separate physical classification from catalogue novelty.

Other pilots were retained with their failures:

* **Near-orbit band:** the first 24, extended to the first 72 cached 12–14.8-day Gaia systems by brightness, excluding fixed40; 72 unique targets and 702 predicted raw windows. Do not add 24+72. The old planet search vetoed this band; the SB1 period fold already searched it. This is an underreviewed/threshold-selected SB1 audit, not a blind recovery from an SB1 veto. TIC 167178300 and TIC 325573958 remain probable target associations because capable neighbours lie 13 and 8 arcsec away. TIC 403769569 is positively off-target (about 35 arcsec deficit offset); its apparent clean repeat is retained as a rejection. Three other strong signals were existing c8-screened EB recoveries. Other threshold coincidences did not establish coherent eclipses.
* **Later-sector repeats:** eight live MAST checks, including the five old unreviewed single/MAYBE appointments objects and three historical single-event examples, found no additional released sectors beyond their cached coverage. This is lack of new data, not physical non-detection.
* **Long duration ceiling:** 16 explicitly selected historical 14-hour maxima were tested at 8,14,18,24,36,48 hours in real raw SAP. Ramps, steps and slow variables dominate; weak single depressions remain unexplained, but no secure repeating long eclipse was established. Large nominal SNR alone did not pass the visual/physical checks.

Selection was adaptive after viewing outcomes, and no success fraction is an unbiased occurrence, completeness or discovery-rate estimate. We stopped broadening after several useful real examples, without relaxing the evidence threshold or promising discoveries.

All five strongest objects have successful fresh seven-group catalogue coverage: VSX, ASAS-SN, SIMBAD, Gaia EB, Prsa2022, IJspeert2024, and vetted Kostov2025 tables3+4. Four have no prior EB identified in that bounded scope. This is not exhaustive literature priority. Raw responses, query timestamps, exact rows, identities and hashes are in catalogues/.

Operational failures were not hidden: the original CDS TIC TAP and its second hostname timed out; six-way IDIA position cones with exact Gaia-ID verification replaced them. Those are execution repairs, not changes to selection or physical labels.
