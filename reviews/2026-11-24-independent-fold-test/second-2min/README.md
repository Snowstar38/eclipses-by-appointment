# Second two-minute search

Ten previously unreviewed apertures were selected from the earlier eligible two-minute sample. Remove the masked pilot, all full-frame TCE-only inputs, Fable's reserved full-frame and changing-eclipse lists, and the current accepted catalogue by exact TIC and position within 30 arcseconds. This leaves 538 apertures. Sort by saved-fold flag, the availability of two or more sectors, and descending depth across adjacent folded bins. The first nine selected apertures have the flag. The tenth is the deepest available unflagged multiple-sector aperture. This selection tests promising leads; it has no matched control arm.

The masked batch was sent to Terra before retrieving any of the 24 TESS sector products. The numerical assessment uses quality-zero cadences from each available archived product, checks the dips in SAP and PDCSAP, and records shoulders, local scatter and contiguous below-threshold samples. The ten per-aperture readings include manual checks of anomalous single dips missed by the multiscale threshold search. Reproduce the selection with draw.py, the archive fetch with sol/archive.py, the numeric triggers with sol/dips.py, and the 17-event clock with sol/clock187.py.

**HD 276904 (TIC 187261476) was the one aperture on which both reviewers identified repeated eclipses.** Seventeen sector 59 minima give a recurrence of 1.17899242 days and a median depth of 3.24%. Two later sectors provide a separate timing test. Using the sector 59 clock, the deepest bin near the predicted minimum is within 0.015 cycles of prediction in both sectors 73 and 86. The depths are 2.9–3.0% in simple-aperture and corrected photometry. The short clock persists across two years. Sector 59 also contains a double-bottomed dip that reaches 40% below the sector median. It stays at least 15% down in both full-frame flux columns for about 13 hours. The same search finds no comparable event during the covered portions of sectors 73 and 86. In the two-minute target pixels, the centers of the light lost in the recurring dips and the deep event differ by about 0.04 TESS pixel. That number is a descriptive fit, without a calibrated centroid uncertainty. The observations support an origin near HD 276904. A third body eclipsing the close pair is one possible explanation. A single deep event gives no outer orbit or dynamical confirmation. [Difference images](pixels/difference.png) · [Pixel measurements and limitations](pixels/findings.md)

The ground check returned 535 ASAS-SN measurements. The faintest points are strongly concentrated on one camera. This series supplied no secure second deep event or independent detection of the short-period signal. [Ground light curves and limitations](ground-187261476/findings.md)

Kostov et al. had already listed this aperture in their machine-selected candidates (Table 2). A separate Gaia eclipsing source 36.79 arcseconds away has a 2.301968-day period. Halving it gives 1.150984 days, which drifts out of phase with the measured minima. The credit search and timing evidence are preserved with this review. This release leaves the host count at its previous value. **Two isolated dips were traced to background subtraction.** For TIC 356752132, a bright feature crossed the edge of the pixel stamp outside the stellar aperture. The elevated background estimate caused a large apparent loss in both published flux columns. For TIC 113655666, restoring the modeled background also removed the dip. In each case the raw aperture counts rose slightly during the apparent eclipse. These pixel checks supersede the initial descriptions of single events in the locked reviewers' tables. They identify a concrete triage test for future isolated dips.
