# Duet's findings on the star paper

Current findings from checks of the release, grouped by topic. Each entry gives the claim, the key numbers, a status (solid, tentative or open) and the files. Proposed table and text changes are collected, one row per change, in [`ledger.csv`](ledger.csv) (built by `ledger.py`; status proposed, favoured, open or for decision). Nothing here edits another table.

Run any script from the repository root, for example `python3 reviews/duet-findings/host-table/nov23_columns.py`; scripts that take arguments say so in their first lines. They need Python 3 with numpy and pandas (Pillow for one figure). Noted exceptions need SciPy, Astropy or rebound, or files kept in the house folder. Every number comes from files in this repository; this environment cannot reach MAST, VizieR, SIMBAD or the Gaia archive.

## Host table (November 23 rows)

- **Fifteen rows adopt 2P with blank secondary columns.** Their own period test measured unequal alternate eclipses, so the secondary exists (R1013 and R1732's notes describe it). Solid. `host-table/nov23_columns.py`, `nov23-column-flags.csv`.
- **101 double-lined rows argue for 2P in text but adopt P.** The heuristic is unsafe blind: R1758 has a real 25 ppt secondary at phase 0.308, so P is right there. Solid; wording is for decision. Same files.
- **21 rows' secondary columns contradict their own rows.** Eight hold the primary's own wing (1.55–1.91 box widths away; no other row is closer than 2.66), others show nothing at the tabled phase, a one-sector dip, a scattered-light ramp (R1789) or a pre-eclipse hump (R997). R1796's column holds 1 − phase; R1349, R1421 and R1459 look mirrored the same way. R1003 (e ≥ 0.51) and R1132 (e ≥ 0.14) are real eccentric systems. Solid. `host-table/secondary_columns.py`, `secondary-column-flags.csv`.
- **25 rows say "no secondary, alternate dips equal" while their visual note describes one.** Measured off the sheets: R1022 0.292, R1029 0.839, R1034 0.544, R1634 0.462, R1727 0.771, R1757 0.746. A secondary at P rules out the twin 2P. R1022's odd cycles fall in the s91 gap (P or 2P both fit); R1727 and R1757 are alias-limited (P ≳ 19 and ≳ 24 d). Solid. `host-table/missed_secondaries.py`, `secondary-seen-not-tabled.csv`.
- **Most equal-dip rows are twins at 2P.** For 121 rows adopting P on equal dips, a main-sequence companion able to make the eclipse would make a visible secondary; P is excluded for 44 rows under strict assumptions (with R1253 and R1697 at 2P because their alternate eclipses differ, not as twins) and 21 more favour 2P under default ones. Calibrated on 500 rows whose secondary fixes the period; agrees with Earthshine's parity test on 12 of 13 shared rows. R1042, R1352 and R1405 are open (reviewers saw a secondary, or P is not excluded). R1684 is P (Earthshine: 1.7 ppt secondary at 0.520). R1040 stays at P. Solid for the 44; tentative for the 21. `host-table/invisible_secondary.py`, `two_rows.py`, `twins_premise.py`, `equal-dips-2P-test.csv`.
- **Eight equal-dip SPOC rows carry a TCE at twice their period.** R991, R1084, R1120 and R1529 are 2P (Earthshine parity); R946's text should give its adopted 0.942508 d (Gaia SB2); R1090 favours 2P (17.1167 d); R1556 is open. Solid. `host-table/tce_period_check.py`.
- **R1737 favours 80.93 d**: a 252 ppt eclipse at 40.46 d needs a 96 ppt secondary that is not there. Tentative. `host-table/twins_premise.py`.
- **`duration_h` is the detection box width, not an eclipse duration.** 459 of 855 values sit on 17 grid values; Earthshine's fitted T14 is 0.41–0.83 times the table value for eight rows. All eight then fit their hosts: R1628 and R1758 are eccentric; R1505 (T14 2.56 h against a 4.71 h circular minimum, no secondary) needs e sin ω ≳ 0.3 or a smaller host, so it is open until an RV orbit. R1034's adopted eclipse is about 100 ppt (tabled 296.75); the deeper, 156 ppt, eclipse is at phase 0.544. Solid. `host-table/earthshine-duration-results.csv`.
- **`depth_ppt` is the mean depth inside the box**: 24% below the deepest point for V-shaped eclipses, 2% for flat ones, 13% typically; 23 rows (21 probable) differ from Earthshine's fit by more than 2.5×. Solid. `host-table/depth_column.py`.
- **R1512's 2.2 ppt occultation is 3.5× too shallow** for any main-sequence companion able to make its 99 ppt eclipse in a circular orbit. Open. `host-table/shallow_secondaries.py`.

## Novelty

- **49 accepted systems are in Chopard-Vilhem's public September list** (exact Gaia DR3 matches; 45 confirmed, 4 probable), and all still count toward "no prior eclipsing-binary identification". 43 were in the snapshot his cross-match used; 6 were never reconciled (R682, R715, R737, R786, R809, R831). Taking them out gives 1,944 (1,418 confirmed, 526 probable). Solid; the fix is for decision. `novelty/chopard_overlap.py`.
- **His pipeline recovered A73 independently.** Seven dips, vetted on target, all fall on the paper's 17.538 d ephemeris (one is a secondary), not on his 124.72 d Gaia period, so his predicted dates are not eclipse times. His 12.8–16.7 d half-windows always contain one of A73's eclipses (every 8.77 d), so a window hit says nothing about the Gaia orbit. His SPOC depths show the same fade, 2–3× shallower than QLP. Solid. `novelty/a73_chopard_dips.py`.

## Gaia errors and the appointment windows

- **About one Gaia DR3 period in twenty misses by 3σ.** Counted without tier, 7 of 199 R systems; 12 of 184 in the A sample. Counting confirmed systems only is circular, since a failed Gaia check was a reason to grade probable. Solid. `gaia-errors/gaia_pulls.py`, `tier_reasons.py`.
- **Gaia's SB2 periods are the weak ones**: 8 of 29 SB2s against 5 of 155 SB1s are 3σ off (p = 1e-4). Solid. `gaia-errors/sb1_vs_sb2_periods.py`.
- **Gaia's correlated conjunction errors run small.** On the 184 same-period A systems, at Gaia's epoch, the published correlations are 1.3–1.7× too small (about 3× for SB2s); independent errors are about 5× too big. Windows from the correlations at 3σ would miss 11 of the 184 found eclipses, 7 of them Table 4 orbits. The search's windows match a 4.5–6σ correlated window (97–108% of its total width), about as wide as needed, so they should not be narrowed. On the R sample, full-covariance times are close to calibrated (robust width 0.70 over 476 measured epochs; 1.29 at Gaia's own epoch over 167). Solid. `gaia-errors/a_epoch_test.py`, `search_window_budget.py`, `window_covariance.py`, `gaia_epoch_test.py`; Fable 5.1's `fable51-*.csv`.
- **R716, R752, R787 and R789 are contradicted Gaia orbits.** Carried back to Gaia's epoch their eclipses land within 1–5 h of Gaia's conjunctions (R814 too; chance about 10⁻⁹ for all five), but their Gaia periods are 3σ off: right binary, wrong Gaia period. Table 4 would have 24. R810 does not fit; R771 is undecided. Solid. `gaia-errors/gaia_epoch_test.py`.
- **A29's conjunction B also misses at Gaia's epoch (1.7 d).** Solid. `gaia-errors/a_epoch_test.py`.
- **R systems held at probable for conjunction tension** (R740, R816, R723, R755, R833) carry no more of it than a dozen confirmed A systems. For decision. `gaia-errors/r_tension_vs_a.py`.
- **R729** needs e ≥ 0.50 like Gaia's SB2 but misses its conjunctions and ω. Open. `gaia-errors/r729_epoch.py` (Fable 5.1's event times).
- **GD1 and GD2** fold cleanly onto Gaia's conjunction A with no odd/even difference. Solid. `gaia-errors/gd_fold.py` (light curves are in the house folder, not here).

## Gaia geometry

- **Gaia's ω predicts which eclipse TESS sees.** In 438 of 491 systems with unambiguous timing the primary falls on the conjunction where Gaia's visible star is eclipsed. Exceptions are evolved visible stars, near-equal companions (2% rising to 71% with mass and radius ratio), or eccentric orbits eclipsing near periastron only. About 15% (68% range 5–26%) of the probable Gaia-fold tier look mis-associated. Gaia eccentricities below about 0.1 under 8 d are mostly noise. Solid. `gaia-geometry/conjunction_kind.py`, `durations.py`, `misassociation.py`; 28 systems to look at in `kind0-check-list.csv`.
- **Six R-system Gaia orbits cannot host their eclipses.** Of 683 accepted systems with both periods, 667 match (664 directly, 3 at a harmonic). Of the 16 that don't, the paper already treats A45, A54 and A113 as wrong Gaia periods. Of the 13 R systems, R732, R753, R757, R788, R792 and R798 cannot be the inner pair of the Gaia orbit (Mardling–Aarseth); R731, R739, R778, R785, R790, R969 and R1003 are allowed triples. Solid. `gaia-geometry/gaia_vs_eclipse.py`, `gaia_mismatch_stability.py`.
- **Companion size supports the association.** For 463 Gaia-fold systems with a usable orbit and depth, Gaia's mass function allows a companion able to make the eclipse, bar one marginal giant; it cannot pick out the probable tier's blends. Solid. `gaia-geometry/companion_size.py`.
- **R490** has the sample's largest mass function (1.29 M☉) and a primary on the companion's conjunction: probably a giant with a hot, B-type companion. Tentative; a spectroscopy target.
- **Unconfirmed single dips near the ecliptic**: "plausible" verdicts are twice as common within 20° (13 of 36 against 56 of 344), but only 1.8σ once coverage is allowed for; 14 signals listed for a moving-object check. Tentative. `gaia-geometry/ecliptic_plausible.py` (needs SciPy and Astropy), `near-ecliptic-plausible.csv`.

## Tidal circularisation

- **Short binaries are circular.** None of 94 secondaries below 2 d has |e cos ω| > 0.031; the eccentric fraction reaches about 70% beyond 12 d; the envelope opens near 3 d. The paper has no circularisation discussion. Solid. `circularisation/circularisation.py`, `ecosw-vs-period.svg`.
- **Hot binaries stay eccentric closer in.** At 4 ≤ a/R₁ < 10, stars at or above 6,250 K are eccentric in 10 of 46 cases against 5 of 178 cool ones (p = 7e-5; 7 of 46, p = 0.0035, without doubtful secondaries); the midpoint moves from about 9.1 to 4.2 d (about 2σ). Tentative. `circularisation/temperature_split.py` (Fable 5's temperature join).

## Triples

- **A73 has a heavy, dark tertiary on a near-circular orbit.** Gaia's e_out = 0.41 is unstable within years. Fits of the whole secondary-eclipse history with e_out 0.10–0.15, a mutual inclination of a few degrees and Gaia's photocentre amplitude (0.719 mas) give m₃ ≈ 0.85 M☉, faint (a white dwarf or a close low-mass pair), and reproduce the phase swing, single-cycle periods and fade. TESS next looks in sectors 123–124 (about August–September 2028): the heavy fits predict a secondary of 121–130 ppt at phase 0.51–0.52, the light ones 86–107 ppt, so the depth decides. Tentative. `triples/a73_fit.py`, `a73_polish.py`, `a73_assess.py`, `a73_forecast.py`, `tertiary_mass.py` (rebound and SciPy for the fits and forecast).
- **R1003's eclipses switched on in 2023–26, faster than its tertiary can explain.** The table row averages over eclipse-free sectors (epoch in s65; tabled P 39σ off the 2026 P = 2.8787113 d; tabled depth a quarter of 2026's 144 ppt). Gaia's photocentre orbit (206.6 d) allows a 0.23 or a 0.86 M☉ tertiary. The impact parameter must have fallen by ≥ 0.33–0.38 (R₁+R₂) from May 2023 to February 2026, ≥ 0.125 per year, but those tertiaries manage at most 0.038 and 0.106 per year. Through 2026 the depths are flat (−7 ± 17 ppt per year where a steady sweep needs about +80). The 2026 timings cap a fourth body at 8–17 d to 5–12 Jupiter masses. So the cause is non-geometric, or b is turning round now; the next TESS sectors and RVs (systemic semi-amplitude about 17 against 5 km/s) decide. Solid for the numbers, open for the cause. `triples/r1003_triple.py`, `r1003_heavy_root.py`, `r1003_node_scan.py`, `r1003_nbody_numpy.py`, `r1003_depth_forecast.py`, `r1003_etv_window.py`, `fourth_body_window.py`; Fable 5's measurements in `triples/fable-r1003/`.
- **R755's eclipses also appeared during TESS.** Its 300.8 d Gaia orbit (e = 0.26) is a stable tertiary; third-body timing wander is hours, and a prediction from two eclipses misses by a median 8–25 h after about two years (up to 66 h for a 1 M☉ tertiary), so the paper's ±0.05 d windows should be about ±1 d. Tentative. `triples/triple_nbody.py`, `r755_prediction_miss.py` (rebound), `r755-prediction-miss-output.txt`. R1000 and R1549 are the best other November 23 rows for timing variations (`triples/tertiary_orbits.py`).
- **R1117's depth-ratio drift is not an inclination change**; spots or a forced eccentricity. Its period is 2.636664 d (eclipse B shallower in 27 of 27 sectors). Solid. `triples/r1117_geometry.py`.
