## Q011, TIC 246892556

Raw SAP has repeated short losses at approximately 6.55 d intervals in sectors 29 and 69, substantially different from the 35.673188 d Gaia spectroscopic clock. Representative sector 69 flags are 3184.6422, 3191.1910, 3197.7397 and 3204.3161 BTJD. These are screening-window coordinates, not fitted eclipse epochs. Sector 29 windows near 2089.5918, 2096.1475 and 2109.2727 are consistent with repeated losses; the additional 2092.3072 window was included in this broad stack and need not be the same phenomenon. The actual light curve supports a repeat but the measured clock, geometry and source need separate care.

Independent TESSCut 3x3 target-centered aperture yields event-minus-side changes of 2.80 ppt (S29) and 3.55 ppt (S69), so a loss persists in a second extraction of the same photons. WCS target pixel (zero based) is (5.459,5.697) in S29, (5.240,5.924) in S69. Positive loss centroids within three pixels are (5.342,6.011) and (5.498,6.495), respectively. The S29 pattern peaks at the target, but S69 exhibits positive and negative structure, so this centroid is descriptive and does not establish a precise location.

The 126-arcsec MAST Gaia DR3 cone includes source 2319058524800146688 at 21.74 arcsec, G=15.019989 versus target G=9.685971. Its approximate G-band flux ratio is 0.00735; a sufficiently deep eclipse can contribute a ~0.005 loss before accounting for actual aperture throughput and color. Its S29/S69 WCS positions are (4.618,6.484)/(4.414,6.726), around 1.15 pixels from target. Two additional approximate capable sources are 61.3 arcsec (G=14.25718, ratio 0.01484) and 56.1 arcsec (G=15.28238, ratio 0.00577). Aperture throughput may reduce their contributions. Difference images favor an origin near the target but do not resolve the closest capable neighbor. Do not attribute uniquely or infer that the photometric and Gaia clocks describe the same binary without further checks. A source-specific PSF fit or resolved photometry could decide attribution.

## Q015, TIC 352610732

The repeated approximately 2.89 d raw SAP losses are real in the extracted light, but independently stacked TESSCut loss images identify a bright, spatially distinct edge source: S41 difference peak near (10,2–3), versus target WCS (5.553,5.493); S55 peak near (2,10), target (5.011,5.903); S82 peak near (3,9), target (5.997,5.004). Across these orientations the peaks correspond roughly to RA 306.565 deg, Dec +55.000 deg. The bright peak corresponds to Gaia DR3 2186586958712163712 at RA 306.5649892584 deg, Dec +55.0006654196 deg, G=9.378956, approximately 99 arcsec from the queued target. This is contrary evidence for assigning these events to the queued target; it does not exclude separate variability or binarity of the target. Target-centered 3x3 differences are -12.22, +0.79 and +0.92 ppt in S41, S55 and S82, and should not be treated as source localization on their own. Stacks use retained window predictions and broad cores rather than fitted centers; the overwhelming off-target difference peak in all three sectors is the decisive observation. Numeric centroid is clipped to three pixels around target, so it understates the location of the far-edge source; use the maps, not that centroid, to locate it.
