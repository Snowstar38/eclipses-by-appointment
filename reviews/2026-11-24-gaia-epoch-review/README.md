## Selection

Gaia's spectroscopic orbit predicts two conjunction phases. The search tested whether at least two Gaia visits inside either window were faint relative to the out-of-window median, with the second-faintest in-window visit more than three scaled median absolute deviations below the baseline and fainter than any outside visit. It also tested quarter-phase and random-phase windows. The TESS follow-up used independent observing epochs. [DR3 orbital solutions with covariance](gaia-orbits.csv) · [Gaia G-band visit magnitudes and quality flags](gaia-visits.csv)

The wider-window experiment repeated the original search on the same 15,981 sources, correcting the conversion of orbital separation to solar radii. The previous expression gave separations 4.21 times too large before a separate uncertainty floor was applied. Among the 12,857 sources outside the DR3 eclipsing-binary table, conjunction hits increased from 836 to 1,034 and quarter-phase control hits increased from 471 to 606. Nine additional conjunction hits reached a score of ten, cleared the control windows and passed the variability-class cuts. The control windows can include an eclipse when widened. The increase in controls and the deliberate follow-up selection limit interpretation of survey yield. [Comparison counts](window-control-counts.csv) · [Nine selected sources and scores](wider-window-selection.csv)

## Measuring periods and locating the eclipses

For the first 15 targets, quality-zero TESS SAP flux was measured against a local linear baseline for each event. A linear fit of eclipse time against integer cycle gave the period. The reference epoch is a fitted minimum near the observations. The event table retains accepted and rejected timings. The first-sample geometry comparison extrapolates the TESS fit back to the Gaia epoch. A label of primary refers to the tracked eclipse. Where two eclipses have similar depths, either conjunction can supply that minimum. [Per-event timings](first-sample-timings.csv) · [Comparison with Gaia conjunctions](first-sample-geometry.csv)

For the nine further targets, up to four sectors per star span the available observations. SPOC or TESS-SPOC light curves were preferred where available. The light curves were normalized by a two-day running median within observing segments, binned at 20-minute resolution, and searched over six Gaia period errors on either side of the published period. The test measured eclipse-like structure at that orbit. Box widths and depths refer to the fitted search box. These periods have no formal photometric errors in this release. All TESS epochs use BJD TDB minus 2,457,000. The input Gaia G-band times retain their original DR3 convention of JD TCB minus 2,455,197.5. [TESS folds](wider-window-folds.png) · [Box fits](wider-window-box-fits.csv) · [Event measurements](wider-window-events.csv) · [Comparison with Gaia conjunctions](wider-window-geometry.csv)

Each tested sector's in-eclipse deficit was fitted at the Gaia target position, at nearby stars bright enough to account for the signal, and at a free position. A positive neighbour-minus-target chi-square difference favours the target. The localization table gives the centroid offset, signal-to-noise, bright alternatives, applied phase, archive product and SHA-256 receipt. For GD1, GD9 and GD11, the deciding images use the refitted clock and per-cadence local sky subtraction. Initial visual readings and comparisons at offset phases are included. TESS reductions of the same observing sector share the same photons. ## Source limitations and earlier records

* R1806 (GD4) has a viable neighbour 7.95 arcseconds away. Gaia measured the target fading in G, BP and RP on three TESS-predicted visits. The neighbour has no published epoch series in this comparison. R1808 (GD6) has a companion 3.77 arcseconds away. On two simultaneous Gaia visits the target faded by 28–37 mmag across the three bands while the companion changed by at most 9 mmag. Both visits have the Gaia noisy-data flag. TESS blends each pair. Both hosts remain probable. [Same-visit measurements and quality flags](close-pair-visits.csv)
* R1818 (TIC 209153902) revises an earlier attribution to a neighbour. In sectors 99, 100 and 102 the deficit lay within 4.4 arcseconds of the target in three different cameras. Sector 11 was unresolved and the absolute position in sector 38 depends on the field registration. R1819 (TIC 370310640) is probable. Three sectors favour the target and one gives a 19.45-arcsecond offset. Its eclipse is 7.97 hours early against the Gaia conjunction, about 6.2 propagated standard deviations. Its association with the spectroscopic orbit remains open. [Wider-window image fits](pixel-localizations.csv) · [Conjunction residuals](wider-window-geometry.csv)
* GD7 is the IJspeert et al. eclipsing binary TIC 173207470. GD12 is TIC 212684258, a strong candidate in Théo Chopard-Vilhem's published spectroscopic-binary records with four detections in four primary windows. Two sectors of localized TESS eclipses support his candidate. Four wider-window targets have prior VSX eclipsing classifications and one is the catalogue's published correction B7. ## Reproducing the measurements

Run `python check_measurements.py` with Python 3.11 or newer to check the host and review joins, refit the 13 accepted first-sample periods and epochs from the retained event times, and reproduce the tier counts. To repeat all nine wider-window box fits, install NumPy, SciPy and Astropy, retrieve their selected light curves from the manifest, and run `python check_measurements.py --fits-dir PATH`. The script verifies the original FITS hashes before fitting. [Check script](check_measurements.py) · [TESS light-curve downloads and SHA-256 hashes](tess-photometry.csv)

The compressed NumPy image archive stores the out-of-eclipse scene and deficit under each published array prefix with suffixes `_out` and `_diff`. The arrays cover the first sample. Retrieve a wider-window cutout from its sector, coordinates and aperture size in the image-fit table. TESScut may repackage regenerated FITS data, so a newly generated ZIP can have a different container checksum. For pipeline reports the checksum is for the uncompressed XML. [TESS archive](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/tess) · [Gaia DR3 epoch-photometry documentation](https://gaia.aip.de/metadata/gaiadr3/epoch_photometry/)
