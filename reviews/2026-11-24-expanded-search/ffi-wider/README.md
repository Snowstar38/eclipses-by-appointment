# Second full-frame archive tranche, 24 November 2026

Six confirmed and four probable photometric eclipsing systems enter the project catalogue from this tranche. Every added system had an earlier public TESS-SPOC threshold-crossing event. These classifications supply eclipse interpretation and host vetting. The 14 additional shortlisted targets and their dispositions are available for comparison.

## Reading the evidence

- `hosts.csv` gives the ten adopted systems in the project import schema. Periods are in days, depth is in parts per thousand, and epoch is BTJD (BJD TDB minus 2,457,000). The two tiers describe evidence for photometric eclipses at the identified system. The measurements cannot resolve individual components in an unresolved multiple.
- `dispositions.csv` gives the outcome and reason for each of 24 systems, including two previously published EBs, two earlier field signals, one human-submitted planet candidate with an EB interpretation, and nine holds. The listed periods for held targets describe the measured modulation.
- `sources/<TIC>.json` contains the two archived DV summaries, original product URLs and SHA-256 digests, four light-curve extraction summaries, measured event centers, depths and local scatter, separate phase-specific pixel fits, Gaia neighbours, independent survey results and previous classifications. Catalogue queries and aliases are recorded per source. Each positional fit reports its verdict and free photocenter offset, possible neighbours and flux-capable unresolved sources.
- `folds-1.png` and `folds-2.png` show the 24 candidates. Each line is a sector, the horizontal axis is cycle phase, and the vertical axis is median-normalized flux in parts per thousand. Phase zero is the trial primary minimum. Panel periods are the initial folding periods. The CSV reports periods fitted to event centers.
- `field-selection.csv` gives the fixed rank, available sectors and processing stage for each of 3,200 fields. `funnel.json` gives stage counts. `catalogue-controls.json` records the positive control and response digest for each catalogue batch.

## Selection and measurements

This tranche covers one-based ranks 1,601 through 4,800 in the repeated-sector full-frame inventory. The initial positional catalogue screen used 32 groups of 100 fields across 11 tables. All groups recovered their controls. The 1,549 positions that passed had archived first- and last-sector DV summaries reviewed. The two sectors had to yield depths above 3,500 ppm, at least two events and multiple-event statistic of at least 9, periods agreeing within 0.2%, photocenter offsets within 12 arcsec, and depths within a factor of 2.5. This gave 250 repeated signals. Existing TESS detections determined the sample and its sensitivity.

The first repeated pair for each target was tested for significant odd-even depth alternation or an independently significant secondary at a consistent phase in both sectors. This led to detailed measurements for 62 fields. The 24 shortlisted signals passed in two sectors and in both TESS-SPOC and QLP raw aperture photometry, two reductions of the same exposures. An odd-even case uses twice the threshold-crossing period. The two-extraction record lists primary event medians for each cycle parity and any separately detected secondary.

The timing fit uses four or five sectors spread across the available baseline. Events had to show measurable fading against both adjacent shoulders, with usable cadence coverage. The primary period fits event centers against integer cycle count. The depth is the median accepted primary depth. An event's cycle index is relative to the trial epoch; the fitted epoch is shifted to a central cycle. The phase panels preserve the trial epoch. Independent ASAS-SN and ZTF photometry was tested at the TESS ephemeris where available.

## Prior work and host identification

A first set of difference images used the archived TCE ephemerides. A second set treated primary and secondary events individually. Bright giants with implausibly short putative orbits, fields with a matching published signal, changing double waves and unresolved eclipse-capable neighbours were inspected individually. The probable tier records any remaining physical or localization ambiguity.

The credit search included the complete 2021 and 2024 IJspeert tables by exact TIC and within 90 arcsec, the Tang eccentric-EB table by exact TIC, the Prša and IJspeert 2024 catalogues within 15 arcmin at matching periods and aliases, Gaia DR3 variability and orbit tables, VSX through HEASARC with Algol as a control, SIMBAD reference titles, NASA ADS full-text alias searches, Villanova, ExoFOP, SuperWASP and project and Théo inventories. The wide-period comparison allowed a fractional difference of 0.002 at period ratios 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4. Optical catalogues and indexed literature have incomplete coverage. The Kostov table 2 classification denotes an unvetted machine detection.
