## Q051: isolated S98 morphology and source

The 4029.150 screening center is not a measured conjunction. Raw flux declines steeply near 4028.5, continues to slope through the low region, then rises sharply near 4029.9. The 4028.67–4029.63 nominal core has 409/416 expected quality-zero cadences; the shape is not created simply by a core gap. The steeply sloping baseline makes an eclipse interpretation insecure. SYS_RM preserves the shape; DET largely removes it, but suppression alone is not proof of instrumental origin. S98 4010.620 is a rise following a 4009.817–4010.042 gap, not a matched closed eclipse. S5 1451.746 starts after a 1449.954–1451.683 gap and only 39/78 core cadences exist, with no left baseline; it is not a sensitive null. S32 Gaia-predicted center 2193.396 intersects a 0.167-day gap: do not use it as a sensitive null for a narrow event. Current TESSCut availability receipt lists S5/S32/S98, not a search of new events.

A fresh MAST 15x15 S98 TESSCut is in helper-tesscut (ZIP retained). Using WCS, target position is (7.796, 7.921) in zero-based cutout pixels. Median pre 4028.10–4028.35, core 4028.85–4029.35, and post 4030.05–4030.30 quality-zero frames contain 108, 215, 108 cadences. The background-adjusted pre/post average minus core difference has positive-flux centroid (7.777, 7.667) within a four-pixel radius: spatially consistent with the target. This is a rough difference on a varying baseline, not a unique localization or proof of eclipse. See helper-pixels.py/json/png. The ESA Gaia DR3 cone query (0.045 deg, G<17; helper-gaia.py/csv) returned a potentially capable neighbor 3289139217167840000 at 37.5 arcsec, pixel (6.82, 6.43), G=14.6496, approximate Gaia G flux ratio 0.0309 relative to target. Its possible total disappearance could produce a percent-scale blended loss, depending on aperture and bandpass. A second G=14.963 source lies 78 arcsec away. Thus blended neighbor ownership is not excluded; the difference centroid is suggestive, not conclusive. PLAUSIBLE with no claimed period or measured depth remains appropriate.

These facts support the lead's bounded rejection of the selected profiles, while common-mode count alone would not establish an artifact.

Inspection of all-sector raw-context images for Q054, Q057 and Q060 shows broad, smoothly varying waves and offsets; I found no separate convincing closed eclipse hidden in those displayed data. Q060's S104 flag sits on a decline and gap/step. This does not rule out weak or gapped events, and the physical origin of the broad waves remains unassigned. Q052–Q060 REFUTED should be read as applying to the selected eclipse evidence only.
