# Two eclipse families in TIC 160193535

Thirty-one TESS eclipses in thirteen indexed sectors give a photometric period of **17.1488321 days**. The deeper eclipse has reference mid-time **BTJD 2944.6101382**. The shallower eclipse follows **8.6482104 days** later, at phase **0.5043032**. Median central PDCSAP depths for complete events were 198 and 163 parts per thousand. The fit used the 27 fully observed events. Four additional eclipses were partly observed. The remaining seven predictions fell in gaps with no core cadences. [Timing measurements](sol/events.csv) · [Predictions and coverage](sol/coverage.csv) · [Timing plot](sol/timing.png).

The alternating spacings of 8.6482 and 8.5006 days make a simple half-period clock measurably wrong. Four-phase labeling at double the adopted period can reproduce the same events. The sector data show no consistent even–odd split supporting that interpretation. [Alias tests and method](sol/findings.md).

**The sky position.** In each of sectors 59 and 79 we imaged a primary and secondary independently. All four difference images favor the nominal counterpart, Gaia DR3 1727862804856539264. No other catalogued Gaia source in these fits was bright enough to explain the loss under the adopted flux model. The images locate the variable light within the TESS resolution and leave close or uncatalogued components possible. [Target-pixel fits and hashes](phase-images.json).

**An independently reduced light curve.** MIT QLP simple-aperture flux shows the expected dimming in all nine measured windows across sectors 19, 26, 59 and 79. In sector 26 the shallower eclipse loses 153 ppt in QLP simple-aperture flux, but measures only 0.6 ppt in the detrended KSPSAP column using the same cadences and baseline windows. Both reductions use the same spacecraft exposures. The ground-survey queries returned no usable data. [QLP windows and archive links](qlp-check.json) · [Ground queries](ground-check.json).

**The spectroscopic orbit.** Gaia reports a double-lined spectroscopic solution at 12.13261 ± 0.00104 days, with eccentricity 0.4412 and argument of periastron 359.64°. Three eclipses in sector 19 alone occupy distinctly different phases at that period. Even fitting both eclipse epochs freely at the Gaia period predicts 45 covered blank windows under the documented test. [Gaia source parameters](gaia/gaia_nss.csv) · [Independent phase check](gaia/summary.txt).

There is also a geometric test independent of the periods measured in days. For an exactly edge-on Keplerian orbit with the published Gaia elements, the two conjunctions occur 0.7715 cycles apart, or 0.2285 if their labels are reversed. The measured photometric separation is 0.5043. Varying eccentricity and argument of periastron across a rectangle of three published marginal errors gives 0.736–0.805, or 0.195–0.264 with swapped labels. These are sensitivity ranges under the edge-on assumption. [Calculation](geometry.py) · [Numerical results](geometry.json).

The data establish an eclipsing clock at the Gaia position. A second unresolved binary and an incorrect Gaia orbital solution are competing explanations; these measurements do not establish multiplicity. Time-resolved spectroscopy spanning both proposed periods would distinguish velocity components tracking the eclipses from components tracking the 12.13-day solution. This follow-up adds timing and attribution evidence to the [independent fold test](../README.md).
