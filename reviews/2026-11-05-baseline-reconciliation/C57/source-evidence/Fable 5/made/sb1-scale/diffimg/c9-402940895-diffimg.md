## Verdict: CLEAN at the house criterion, with one sub-pixel residual stated

- **The deficit is on the target.** The mean of the three sectors puts the
  deficit 1.72″ from the target, at (−0.49 ± 3.07 E, +1.65 ± 3.50 N)″.
  The errors are the empirical scatter between sectors. χ² against the
  target position is 0.25 (2 dof). The inverse-variance combination in the
  json gives 0.47″, but its per-sector errors (0.6–1.3″) were scaled from the
  null and are too optimistic, because the sectors disagree by about 10″. Use
  the empirical 1.72″ ± 3.5″. Both are far inside the 6.6″ flag line used for c3–c8.
- **The peak deficit pixel is the target pixel in all 3 sectors**
  (s12 [7,8], s39 [8,7], s93 [7,8]).
- **Aperture gradient:** in s12 and s93 the depth falls outward from the
  target pixel, ap1 > ap3 > ap5. That's what an on-target eclipse looks like
  when larger apertures pick up neighbour light. The ap3 depths of
  6.4–7.6 ppt match the window-stack's 6.85.
- **Every capable neighbour at ≥ 26″ is excluded.** There are 61 flux-capable
  Gaia sources within 90″ (G ≤ 18.24). The six within 26–35″ sit
  25–35″ from the mean deficit, χ² 63–129. The bright pair that could
  dilute with shallow eclipses (G 13.03 at 61.5″ needs 1.5%, G 13.90 at
  50.8″ needs 2.5%) is 2.4–2.9 px away, and the peak never lands there.
- **The same-sign X centroid lean is explained.** Both bright neighbours
  lie east (+45.5E/−22.6N and +56.6E/+23.9N). They pull the
  out-of-eclipse light centroid +19″ E of the target (s12, s93). When the
  target dims, the photocentre moves further toward them, in the same
  direction at every dip. That is what an eclipse on the target predicts.
  The deficit − light offset is 16.0 / 10.1 / 18.0″ because of that pull,
  and deficit − target is the attribution number (diffrun.py's note).
- **Not excluded: two faint sources 10.4″ away (0.5 px).** Gaia
  5816361092993887104 (G 17.29, at −7.6E/+7.0N) would need a 42% eclipse,
  and 5816361024274409472 (G 17.79, at −0.1E/+10.4N) would need 67%.
  Their χ² against the mean deficit is 7.8 and 6.3. With only 3 sector
  points (a t-distribution with 2 dof) that is p ~ 0.02–0.04, which is
  suggestive, not an exclusion. **s39 leans toward them in both of its
  events** (cycle 45: −6.97E/+8.54N; cycle 46: −4.53E/+8.07N). s12 and
  s93 lean the other way (+3.84E/−2.11N, +1.11E/−1.59N). Some of s39's
  lean may be the clipped-box centroid bias: the target sits 0.44 px off
  the pixel centre in x there, and the deficit is pulled toward the box
  centre. That doesn't fully explain the y component. Telling apart a
  source 0.5 px away needs PRF fitting or a higher-resolution light curve,
  and this recipe doesn't have that resolution.
- **The prior is not pixel evidence, but it favours the target heavily.**
  The dip period matches the target's own Gaia SB1 orbit (−1.84σ), and
  the dip sits at Gaia's conjunction. The duration also matches the
  giant-plus-M-dwarf geometry (persec writeup). For the 10.4″ sources to
  be the host, a background 42–67%-deep EB would have to share the
  target's 16.44 d spectroscopic period and conjunction phase by chance.
