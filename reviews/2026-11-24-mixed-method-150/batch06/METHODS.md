# QLP triage methods for batches 06–10

From the review directory, run `python inputs/prepare.py 7` and `python -B batch06/review_lane.py 7` for batch 7 with NumPy, SciPy, Astropy and Matplotlib. Prepare each desired batch before supplying its number to the review script. The script uses common read and plot functions from the earlier batch 2 review. Inputs are the lane batch CSVs, original local QLP light-curve FITS in `sky/data/qlp-sb-p20-60`, archive window reports in `Astra/made/binary-exploration-2026-11-04/bulk-results`, and the camera-aware QLP exposure list in `sky/mixed-fold-2026-11-24/cadence-controls`.

The analysis compares SAP with the available QLP correction (KSPSAP_FLUX or DET_FLUX), keeps finite paired photometry with QUALITY = 0, and excludes the listed camera-specific exposures. Archived flagged dips and each selection anchor are plotted. For each Gaia, pilot or fold-screen period, the provisional phase is selected from archive event times by the number of distinct sectors with near-phase events. This can pick false coincidences and provides a hypothesis for subsequent covered-window checks. The optional hypotheses tables exercise other repeat intervals or fitted centers on the same curves. The TESS phase alignment for the Gaia period uses the observed timing and is not a nominal spectroscopic conjunction prediction.

Window depth is the median deficit against a line between the flux medians in two local wings, in ppt. Covered windows require at least 65% nominal sampling in the core and in each wing, and spacing no larger than the greater of 0.025 d and 15% of window width. The tables include separate counts, the maximum core spacing, and unobserved or partial predictions. Large unphysical depths at gaps can result from fitting across discontinuities. Event plots were inspected for morphology, incomplete ingress and egress, smooth continuous variability, and differences after correction. Lomb–Scargle scans cover periods from 0.125 to 14.3 d and give the top three peaks per sector and per flux product. Selected sector-relative folds test waveform morphology without assuming a shared epoch. The single eclipse candidate also has a common-epoch fold and per-sector phase residuals.

`common-windows.csv` compares flagged archive windows within 0.35 d between targets observed in the same sector across the 50 reviews. A match calls for inspection of shape and observing geometry. Clusters such as the S94 event around 3874.9 BTJD appear on multiple cameras and provide evidence of shared observing disturbances. The special 14 Peg comparison aligns spacecraft cadence IDs to ensure both stars are measured at the same exposure even when barycentric timing differs.
