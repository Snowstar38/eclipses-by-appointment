# Duet's findings on the star paper

Current findings from checks of the release, grouped by topic. Each entry gives the claim, the key numbers, a status (solid, tentative or open) and the files. Proposed table and text changes are collected, one row per change, in [`ledger.csv`](ledger.csv) (built by `ledger.py`; status proposed, favoured, open or for decision). Nothing here edits another table.

Run any script from the repository root, for example `python3 reviews/duet-findings/host-table/nov23_columns.py`; scripts that take arguments say so in their first lines. They need Python 3 with numpy and pandas (Pillow for one figure). Noted exceptions need SciPy, Astropy or rebound, or files kept in the house folder. Every number comes from files in this repository; this environment cannot reach MAST, VizieR, SIMBAD or the Gaia archive.

## Gaia errors and the appointment windows

- **About one Gaia DR3 period in twenty misses by 3σ.** Counted without tier, 7 of 199 R systems; 12 of 184 in the A sample. Counting confirmed systems only is circular, since a failed Gaia check was a reason to grade probable. Solid. `gaia-errors/gaia_pulls.py`, `tier_reasons.py`.
- **Gaia's SB2 errors leave out their own poor fit; scaled by it, they are right.** As published, 8 of 29 same-period SB2s against 5 of 155 SB1s miss the eclipse period by 3σ (p = 1e-4; robust width 2.31). The SB2 fits are poor (median goodness of fit 7.5, up to 28.8; √(χ²/ν) median 2.1, 0.91–5.3), and dividing each pull by its own √(χ²/ν) brings the period pulls to width 1.12 with none beyond 3σ, and the conjunction pulls at Gaia's epoch from width 2.95 (10 beyond 3σ) to 1.26 (3): A29 (−6.2σ; its conjunction B misses too), A167 (+4.3σ; a third body in its timing) and A89 (+3.5σ). The scaling holds on a catalogued eclipser outside the sample: TIC 236647148's eclipses in s16 and s83 are 36 cycles of 50.9259 d, 12.7σ from Gaia's 50.8905 ± 0.0028 d as published and 1.9σ with its √(χ²/ν) of 6.8 (Earthshine; Search yield). Solid. `gaia-errors/sb1_vs_sb2_periods.py`, `fit_quality_scaling.py`.
- **Gaia's correlated conjunction errors run small.** On the 184 same-period A systems, at Gaia's epoch, the published correlations are 1.3–1.7× too small (about 3× for SB2s); independent errors are about 5× too big. Windows from the correlations at 3σ would miss 11 of the 184 found eclipses, 7 of them Table 4 orbits. The search's windows match a 4.5–6σ correlated window (97–108% of its total width), about as wide as needed, so they should not be narrowed. On the R sample, full-covariance times are close to calibrated (robust width 0.70 over 476 measured epochs; 1.29 at Gaia's own epoch over 167). Solid. `gaia-errors/a_epoch_test.py`, `search_window_budget.py`, `window_covariance.py`, `gaia_epoch_test.py`; Fable 5.1's `fable51-*.csv`.
- **Gaia's eccentricity errors are also about 1.4× too small, and near-circular fits run high.** At P < 3 d, where the eclipses show the orbits are circular, the free-eccentricity SB1/SB2 fits give e/σ with a Rayleigh scale of 1.37 ± 0.12 (31 systems; 1.44 ± 0.17 for 17 below 2.5 d) instead of 1; R467 and R607 are above 3σ, and R607's e = 0.32 ± 0.05 at 2.13 d, with no secondary measured, looks like a failed fit like R1819's. For 14 A systems whose secondary phase the paper states, Gaia's |e cos ω| against the phase's gives rms pulls of 1.35 (1.80 with A145, whose Gaia e = 0.038 ± 0.008 is 4.7σ from a circular orbit at phase 0.49996), and 11 of the 14 pulls are positive. The two clearly eccentric ones agree closely (A123 0.224 against 0.226, A151 0.098 against 0.093). The same factor as the conjunction-time errors. Tentative (small samples). `gaia-errors/eccentricity_errors.py`.
- **Most of Table 4's e cos ω contradictions are what Gaia's understated errors produce by chance.** With eccentricity errors 1.37 ± 0.12 times the quoted ones, 184 compared orbits should give 5.2 (2.9–8.1) chance 3σ failures in e cos ω; Table 4 has 8. Scaled by each SB1's own goodness of fit (next entry), A21, A63, A159 and A202 (F2 1.8–2.8) fall to 1.7–2.2σ (the paper's A159 note already suspects understated errors from its goodness of fit of 2.6), while A145 (F2 −1.27) stays at 4.6σ and A65 (F2 −0.53) at 3.6σ: those two are well-fitted solutions that the eclipses contradict, and both also miss their conjunctions at Gaia's epoch (3.3σ and 3.1σ after scaling). A28 and A30 are SB2s, with goodness of fit 14.9 and 23.7, so their contradictions sit on poorly fitted solutions (with a flat 1.37: 4.2σ and 3.9σ). The period failures are different: the A sample's period pulls have a robust width of 1.16 and do not follow the goodness of fit, so 1.8 chance failures against 13 observed is a real excess, though for the R systems tested the orbit is still the right binary (next entries). Tentative. `gaia-errors/table4_scaled.py`, `fit_quality_scaling.py`.
- **Gaia's SB1 errors are right for well-fitted orbits and grow with the goodness of fit.** Conjunction pulls at Gaia's epoch (full covariance) widen with the solution's F2: robust width 1.05, 1.30 and 2.22 across its terciles for 155 A systems (Spearman p = 0.001), and 1.22, 0.93 and 1.76 for 165 R systems (p = 0.007). It holds within confirmed systems alone (273; widths 1.05, 1.23, 1.88, p = 1e-4), so it is not the tier grading, which used Gaia tension. Over the 320 together the errors need a scale of 1.24 exp(0.18 F2) (slope 0.10–0.22 at 68%, never zero in 300 bootstraps): 1.04× at F2 = −1, 1.5× at 1, 2.1× at 3, steeper than the solutions' own √(χ²/ν), which reaches only about 1.5. It calibrates every F2 quartile (widths 0.86–1.09, against 0.76–1.47 for a flat 1.44× of the same mean). Fitted on conjunctions alone, it also fixes the eccentricity errors: on 30 circular SB1s below 3 d the Rayleigh scale of e/σ is 0.93 below the median F2 and 1.73 above, 0.95 overall once scaled. So the average factors of the entries above (1.3–1.7× on conjunctions, 1.37× on e) are a mix of right errors on good fits and errors about twice too small near F2 = 3, where DR3's SB1 cut stops (Triples). At the same total window width the scale leaves as many 3σ conjunction misses (4 of 320) as a flat factor; those that remain have low F2 (A41, A65, A145, P025), so they mark wrong orbits, not noise. Gaia's period pulls do not follow F2 (width 0.96–1.08 across terciles). The G 11.5–12 finds show the same sign at the TESS epochs (32 with clean repeated eclipses, against the windows' independent errors: width 0.89 at F2 ≤ 1.5, 1.63 for the 5 above; p = 0.20; `yield/fresh_band_yield.py`). Among the accepted R systems' SB1s, F2 runs higher for cooler stars (Spearman −0.21 with Teff, p = 1e-5) and larger semi-amplitudes (+0.15), and does not follow brightness, RUWE, period or eccentricity. Nor does F2 mark the systems whose primary falls on the other conjunction (kind-1 fraction 0.08–0.14 in every F2 bin, 471 SB1s with clean timing) or the probable tier (median 0.78 against 0.88 for confirmed). SB2s are treated above. F2 and obj_func fix the degrees of freedom, which recovers N_RV where an export has placeholders (exact on all 816 SB1 rows that list it). Solid for the trend, tentative for the exact law. `gaia-errors/fit_quality_scaling.py` (SciPy for its last block), `fit-quality-scaling.csv`, `fit-quality-scaling-output.txt`.
- **R716, R752, R787 and R789 are contradicted Gaia orbits.** Carried back to Gaia's epoch their eclipses land within 1–5 h of Gaia's conjunctions (R814 too; chance about 10⁻⁹ for all five), but their Gaia periods are 3σ off: right binary, wrong Gaia period. Table 4 would have 24. R810 does not fit; R771 is undecided. Solid. `gaia-errors/gaia_epoch_test.py`.
- **R750's secondary eclipses come 3.4 d before Gaia's orbit puts them.** Its nine measured eclipses (TIC 33065877, two kinds, 196–339 ppt) put the primaries 0.15–0.18 d from Gaia's conjunction A, but the secondaries come 12.51 d after the primary (phase 0.416) where Gaia's e = 0.444 ± 0.013 and ω = 85.0 ± 1.3° give 15.88 d (0.528 ± 0.007): 15σ, and 5.7–8.6σ with the errors scaled by the solution's √(χ²/ν) (goodness of fit 8.9). Every secondary lies 3.2 d before Gaia's conjunction B, outside its 1.5 d window. Right binary and period, wrong e and ω; with R716, R729, R752, R787 and R789, Table 4 would have 26. Solid. `gaia-errors/r750_secondary.py`, `r750-secondary-output.txt`.
- **A29's conjunction B also misses at Gaia's epoch (1.7 d).** Solid. `gaia-errors/a_epoch_test.py`.
- **R systems held at probable for conjunction tension** (R740, R816, R723, R755, R833) carry no more of it than a dozen confirmed A systems, and with Gaia's errors scaled by each orbit's goodness of fit all five are within 2σ at Gaia's epoch (R833 +1.9, R816 +1.6, R740 −1.6, R755 +1.3, R723 +0.1). For decision. `gaia-errors/r_tension_vs_a.py`, `fit_quality_scaling.py`.
- **R729's Gaia SB2 orbit is wrong.** Its eclipses need e ≥ 0.50 like Gaia's but miss its conjunctions and ω, and its semi-amplitudes give 0.30 + 0.31 M☉ for a 5,800 K, 4.2 L☉ pair (TIC mass 1.05 M☉). Solid. `gaia-errors/r729_epoch.py` (Fable 5.1's event times), `masses/sb2_masses.py`.
- **GD1 and GD2** fold cleanly onto Gaia's conjunction A with no odd/even difference. Solid. `gaia-errors/gd_fold.py` (light curves are in the house folder, not here).

## Search yield

- **At G 11.5–12 the appointment search finds deep eclipses on strong Gaia orbits at the geometric rate, and shallow or long ones rarely.** For the 2,392 science targets of the five frozen fresh-band cohorts, each Gaia row gives the primary's radius (parallax, G and BP−RP, no extinction), the companion's minimum mass and the orbit; random orientations and the windows' coverage then give 155 expected eclipsing systems deeper than 2 ppt, against 61 accepted at Gaia's period (5 more were reviewed at another period). Found over expected is 0.73 for predicted depths of 60 ppt or more, 0.34 for 20–60 ppt and 0.14 below 20 ppt, and 4 of 35 expected for eclipses longer than 30 h (giants). It rises with the orbit's significance: deep eclipses on orbits of significance 30 or more are found 18 times against 18.3 expected, which calibrates the model, but at 0.45 below 15, and shallow ones at 0.07 below 15. A joint Poisson fit gives factors of 0.40 ± 0.11 for shallow and 0.09 for long eclipses, and a found rate rising as significance to the power 0.79 ± 0.21. The trends hold for dwarfs alone (deep 38 of 52 expected, shallow 7 of 36) and for depth floors of 1–5 ppt, giant masses of 1.4–2 M☉, companion radii 20% larger and without the coverage (p ≤ 2e-4 throughout for the excess of deep finds). So about 36 eclipsing systems on orbits of significance 30 or more remain unfound in these cohorts, mostly shallow eclipses and the day-long eclipses of giants; the low-significance deficit comes from review, where a weak orbit cannot fix a single event's cycle (the weak-orbit entry below). Yields per hundred stars therefore depend on each band's share of giants, of small companions and of weak orbits, not on the method alone. Solid for the trends; tentative for the counts (no extinction, main-sequence companions). `yield/fresh_band_yield.py` (SciPy), `fresh-band-yield.csv`, `fresh-band-yield-output.txt`.
- **A full-baseline search of the 60 likeliest unfound orbits accounts for the faint-band deficit with the giants' own variability and the re-search's depth floor, not missed windows.** The 60 orbits of significance 20 or more with the highest chance of an unfound eclipse (`yield/fresh-band-research-list.csv`: 30 long, 8.0 eclipses expected; 30 shallow, 5.0 expected) were searched in every QLP sector at Gaia's conjunctions, with boxes of 12–96 h for the long group and 1–14 h on SAP and KSPSAP flux for the shallow group (Fable 5.1, `reviews/2026-11-26-fable51-fresh60-research/`). Long group: no eclipser and one candidate (TIC 278774793); for 25 of 29 stars the scatter of the box depth at the predicted duration exceeds half the predicted 2–13 ppt depth, because these giants vary by 10–70 ppt on 2–4 d, so a single eclipse of the predicted size cannot stand out. The giants' day-long eclipses are therefore below these stars' own variability, not outside the windows. Shallow group: one eclipser, TIC 403114300 (5–6 ppt, flat, at conjunction A in 11 sectors, P = 28.4298 d against Gaia's 28.4561 ± 0.0153), and one candidate (TIC 398571726). 27 of these 30 stars are subgiants or low giants (R₁ 3–8 R☉), and 24 have predicted eclipse durations above the pass's 14-h maximum box, carrying 4.0 of the 5.0 expected; both dips were first measured at the 14-h cap. The detrended KSPSAP column flattens 10–20 h, 5 ppt eclipses to 0.1–0.3 ppt in some sectors, but the frozen fresh-band screen used raw SAP flux (`screen-seal.json`), so this does not touch the cohorts' counts. Longer boxes alone do not help on SAP flux: with the grid extended to 36 h every star's best box runs to the cap on ramps (SNR 70–700, white-noise σ low by 1.5–16× at these durations) and TIC 403114300 drops out. Sensitivity was not the limit either: the red-noise spread of a single box at each star's predicted duration is 0.2–1.3 ppt against predicted depths of 2–35 ppt. A phase-coherent search (trial periods within ±3σ of Gaia's, all phases, depths stacked over every covered cycle, null from off-conjunction phases; Earthshine) recovers TIC 403114300 blind (Z 39.7 at conjunction A, P = 28.4305 d; off-phase null maximum 14.9) and TIC 398571726 (Z 17.2), and adds one candidate, TIC 195525295 (conjunction A, P = 26.0228 d, about 2.5 ppt over about 18 h, clean in sectors 56, 75 and 83; Z 14.3 against an off-phase maximum of 6.1; Gaia predicts 3.1 ppt over 14.1 h), which the re-search saw (repeated, z 21) and removed at its 5 ppt depth floor. So in the shallow group the binding cut was the depth floor, not the box length. Free-duration fits put TIC 403114300 at about 16–18 h total (5.3–5.5 ppt, in all 14 covered cycles) and TIC 398571726 at 16–24 h, 0.6–0.8 of the predicted 23.8 and 29.4 h. Solid for the long group and the shallow group's binding cut; tentative for TIC 195525295. Data and per-star tables in the Fable 5.1 folder and Earthshine's coherent-search notes in the house folder (`2026-11-26-opus55-fresh60-long`); expectations from `yield/fresh_band_yield.py`.
- **In the G 11.5–12 cohorts the appointment windows add no system to the period-free dip harvest.** The visual sheets that chose the review leads plot only the harvest's dip windows (`triage_fresh*.py`), and all 61 finds at Gaia's period carry a harvest dip flag (contrast ≥ 5, depth ≥ 1 ppt); 49 of them also pass the appointment test, which 31% of all targets pass. The 12 it rejects fail the 14-h maximum-box rule (6, including two of the four giants' day-long eclipses), the far ratio (4), the null windows (4) or the halves test (2). Nine of the 12 fail one of the paper's own tests (far ratio, halves, null windows or SNR); the other three only the triage script's maximum-box rule. A screen of the appointment test alone, like the G < 11 search, would lose 9 of these 61 real eclipses (about one in seven) on the paper's tests alone. The 485 targets that pass the appointment test without a dip flag were never plotted, but their saved window events hold no excess of same-kind dips repeating at a fixed offset from Gaia's conjunction (16 at zero lag against 14–21 at control lags of 0.75–5 d, where the finds give 39 against 3–25), so few repeated eclipses hide there. The cohorts' 66 accepted claims therefore measure a period-free single-dip search followed by the Gaia conjunction check, which finds the single eclipses that a periodic search misses without needing the windows; the paper's "conjunction and raw-event search" does not say which channel the leads came from. Solid. `yield/fresh_band_channels.py`, `fresh-band-channels-output.txt`.
- **The main search's band yields differ partly because bright eclipsers are already catalogued; geometry is the same in every band.** The same model run on the Gaia rows of the 6,357 main-search targets (40 Gaia eclipsing-binary controls left out; window coverage from each star's covered-window count, calibrated on the fresh-band windows) expects 6.4–6.6 eclipsing systems deeper than 2 ppt per hundred stars in every band, with the same share of giants (25–28%). The appointment finds at Gaia's period (Table A1 method App, and the conjunction-window reviews at G 11–11.5; 101 of 104 at Gaia's period) are 14 of 88.8 expected at G < 10 (0.16), 45 of 153.9 at G 10–11 (0.29) and 42 of 172.4 at G 11–11.5 (0.24). G < 10 holds the strongest orbits, so depth, duration and significance alone predict 27.7 finds there against the 14 found (Poisson p = 0.003). The window statistic is not the cause: it reaches z ≥ 5 for the same 48% of strong deep-eclipse stars in every band. A further 81 targets, none accepted, are eclipsing binaries in Prša et al. (2022) or IJspeert et al. (2024) (the identity flag in `reviews/2026-11-05-period-free/selection/all-targets.csv`; 70 from IJspeert alone): 27 at G < 10, 31 at G 10–11, 23 at G 11–11.5. Not all of them are the Gaia orbit eclipsing. Folding each catalogue period and epoch on Gaia's orbit (errata, `yield/catalogue-verdicts.csv`) gives 20 at Gaia's period (14 by period, 6 by phase alone), 19 apparently with a different signal, 28 that Gaia's period error cannot decide and 14 with no catalogue period. Of the 19, six have catalogue periods of 0.12–0.9 d against orbits of 20–60 d, a different signal on period alone. The other 13 rest on the catalogue epoch missing both conjunctions, and 11 of them are SB2 solutions, mostly poorly fitted (13 of the 19 are SB2, against 8% of all targets; their median goodness of fit is 22.6, against 12.8 for all SB2 targets). With Gaia's errors scaled by each solution's fit quality, as the Gaia-errors entries calibrate (SB2 √(χ²/ν), SB1 1.24 exp(0.18 F2)), only 3 of the 13 stay beyond 3σ (TIC 90238281, 236647148 and 289044378), so 9 are robustly another signal and 10 join the undecided; 19 of the 20 same-orbit targets stay within 3σ. TESS pixels and VSX settle those nine (Fable's TESScut fits with injected controls, and VSX cones; `yield/catalogue-pixels.csv`). Four catalogue entries do not describe the target: the Prša 0.536 d eclipse at TIC 266769521 is AA Cet, the G 7.3 star 6.6″ away, and the Prša 0.431 d signal at TIC 379806358 is the W UMa star WISE J170737.5−705248, 41.5″ away, while the IJspeert entries at TIC 148624083 (0.599 and 0.142 d) and 168656470 (0.117 d) show nothing across the frame above 0.3–1 ppt in the sectors they were measured from. Four are Gaia's orbit. TIC 121945407 is HD 181068, the known triply eclipsing triple, whose outer eclipsing orbit is Gaia's 45.47 d (the 0.906 d entry is its inner pair), and TIC 289044378 is V1110 Cas, eclipsing on the target every 24.85 d, half of Gaia's period to 0.03%, so Gaia's e and ω are what is off. TIC 90238281's single deep event (s82, 179 ppt) is Gaia's conjunction A, −0.3σ from the prediction once the period error is carried over the 71 cycles since Gaia's epoch (±1.78 d), and a flat 64 ppt secondary follows 11.92 d later against Gaia's 12.20 ± 0.85 d (Earthshine); its catalogue period is not the orbit. TIC 236647148's event in s83 (92 ppt) recurs in s16, 36 cycles of 50.9259 d earlier; on that period it lands 0.03 d from Gaia's conjunction A at Gaia's epoch, so it is Gaia's orbit with Gaia's period 12.7 formal σ off (1.9σ with the fit-quality-scaled error; Gaia errors). One is another signal on the target: 2.5 ppt at 0.375 d on TIC 279645119 (ASAS-SN lists an eclipsing binary there at 0.4989 d). So 77 of the 81 flagged targets hold an eclipser, 24 of them at Gaia's period; the four that do not have window statistics of −3.1 to 1.7 at Gaia's conjunctions. The search's window statistic z, which none of these tests used, agrees with the split: z ≥ 20 for 19 of the 24 same-orbit targets (the six matched by phase alone, where errata put the chance floor near half, all have z ≥ 17) and for 11 of the 13 no-period targets with a z (2.5 expected at the 19% rate of targets with no known eclipser), and for 6 of 16 undecided and 4 of 10 newly undecided ones (3.1 and 1.9 expected). The no-period entries are mostly long-period eclipsing binaries that the catalogue saw once a year, sitting at Gaia's conjunctions. The verified catalogued eclipsers also calibrate the window test with a set chosen without it: z ≥ 8 for 22 of the 24 same-orbit targets and 12 of 13 no-period ones, against 21 of the 30 Gaia eclipsing-binary controls that have a z (the paper counts 32 of 40 controls with an eclipse at the predicted conjunction). For a dark tertiary on a circular orbit coplanar with the eclipses, the eclipse delay must then reach a semi-amplitude of at least 1.1–1.9 min for R113, R114, R173, R238, R225 and R393 (per-CCD error 0.1 mas; half to twice that over 0.06–0.2 mas), at outer periods of 150–300 d, rising to 1.3–2.3 min at 1,000 d and 6–13 min at 3,000 d. R625's floor is 4.9 min (3.0–9.8), because it is the most distant (parallax 1.1 mas), yet its sector means in 2019 and twice in 2024 span only 1.4 min. Over random orientations (outer period 150–12,000 d, log-uniform), a tertiary that makes R625's RUWE leaves timing that flat in 20% of draws, mostly at low inclination (median 38°). So R625's wobble more likely belongs to an inclined or long-period tertiary, or to a neighbour, than to a coplanar companion of the eclipsing pair. A luminous tertiary would raise every floor. Tentative. `timing/astrometric_timing_floor.py`, `astrometric-timing-floor.csv`, `astrometric-timing-floor-output.txt`.
