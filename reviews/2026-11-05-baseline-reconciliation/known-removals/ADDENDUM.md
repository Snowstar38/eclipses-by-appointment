## R390: prior proposal, not a contrary attribution

The original Tarrants & Mendes 2023 v1 e-print, section III.9, says TOI-2473 has a 1.2312 d candidate and a second candidate at 6.48369 d; it identifies unrelated foreground SB1 2MASS J04573259+1025178, Gaia DR3 3291551648758101248, with Gaia orbital period 6.48369 ± 0.00077 d and proposes that this SB1 is an eclipsing binary blending into the second candidate. It explicitly cannot establish an epoch match because the Gaia transit-window uncertainty is about two-thirds of an orbit. Its table gives TOI-2473 / Gaia 3291551648758101248 / identifier No / period Yes / epoch ? / Blend. Thus the table's 'No' is identifier match, not period match; the earlier audit's brief misread the column. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12380v1, original HTML SHA-256 `be86aa1925214aef5dafed478a637ae6b1dde73752a784bd6c9a11a24f2c6ae6`; exact table extract in `primary-tarrants-rows.txt`. The e-print is publicly available, unrefereed, and itself labelled a draft.

R390's report `[local-path-redacted]` identifies the same Gaia source and measures P=6.4834644669 ± 0.0000125529 d, with sector 98 deficit centroid 0.057 pixel from target and nearest catalogued capable neighbor 1.860 pixels from deficit. The two periods differ 0.0002255 d, about 0.29 Gaia-period standard deviations, and support the same proposed association; the 1.2312 d candidate is a different signal. The source's prior claim already identifies the foreground star as the proposed eclipsing blend, so exclude R390 from *new* discoveries even though it was a hypothesis. Retain it as a confirmed recovery at TESS resolution and credit new localization and characterization. The earlier paper's epoch-match uncertainty remains; this comparison alone does not independently prove the historical candidate epoch is identical, nor exclude unresolved contaminants. Do not describe T&M as having proven or directly localized its proposed blend.

## A74 numeric reproduction

It reproduces 1,589 good target points, four predicted-window points z=4.5, 3.1, 1.6, 2.0 and mean z=2.79, plus 2 of 6,464 sampled phase steps at least that high (both wrap copies of the same epoch). This is a reproducibility check of existing code, not an independently written reduction or validation of its null calibration. The 2/6464 grid fraction is not an independent-trial p-value. Sparse sampling and unestablished secondary remain explicit; the vetter's physical promotion is supported by independent instrument/epochs rather than a resolved ground eclipse.
