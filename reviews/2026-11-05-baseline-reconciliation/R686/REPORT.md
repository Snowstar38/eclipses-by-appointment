Gaia DR3 **1868634515521921152**; SB1, Gaia period **41.804020454205606 ± 0.0106868865 d**, eccentricity **0.01969 ± 0.00869**, G = 11.546069. This is exact queue row 1. The frozen latest-three-sector screen called the smooth sector-76/82 B-like minima `unusual_repeated_signal`; the earlier-sector evidence below was adaptive full review, not part of the screen denominator.

**Physical verdict: probable eclipsing system.** Distinct deeper and shallower sequences recur at the same approximately 41.8-day period and about half an orbit apart. Their raw local profiles have resolved ingress and egress above slower baseline variability. A target-centered sector-76 difference image and a custom TESScut aperture extraction support the Gaia target, but two ~G15.3 neighbors 22–24 arcsec away remain borderline capable in a maximum-depth flux argument, and localization uncertainty has not been calibrated. This unresolved attribution keeps the verdict probable, while the astrophysical repeated eclipse interpretation is strong. Catalogue novelty is root-owned and pending; this report does not count the system as a new addition.

The B cycle intervals are 13 (s56–76) and 4 (s76–82), with a 17-cycle endpoint; the A intervals are 26 (s15–55), 13 (s55–75), and 5 (s75–83). A common-slope fit with **separate A and B epochs** gives descriptive photometric **P = 41.800944 d**, A epoch **3359.489216 BTJD** and B epoch **3380.386471 BTJD**. The A-to-B interval **20.897254 d** differs from half the fitted period by **0.003218 d**. Maximum residual among seven centers is **0.0121 d**. Gaia period differs by 0.00308 d, inside its reported 0.01069 d catalog error. These values test cycle assignments and orbital compatibility; they are **not** calibrated precision ephemerides. Local baseline choices and a 0.013 d QLP-versus-TESScut s76 center shift warrant at least ~0.02 d per-center working allowance. No formal photometric period error is claimed. “B” denotes the deeper observed event under this screening convention, not a secure stellar-component identity.

The source-attribution calculation is deliberately bounded. A sector-76 TESScut deficit centroid is **4.22 arcsec from the Gaia target** and about **20.5 arcsec from the G15.32 neighbor**; the other G15.35 neighbor is at 24.33 arcsec from the target. The difference image visibly peaks at the target pixel. A target-centered 3×3-pixel, border-sky-subtracted light curve from the same cutout gives a **29.84 ppt** broad dip with a **1.496 d** model width, against QLP's **30.14 ppt** and **1.533 d** in its ±2 d fit. These are **different extractions of the same TESS photons**, not independent observations. The closest G15.32 star is 3.77 G magnitudes fainter, which gives about a 3.0% isolated-pair *maximum blended loss* if it vanished entirely in that band. Thus the 2.8–3.0% observed B loss is near the bound, and the G15.35 source is similarly borderline. TESS passband, differing apertures, additional blends and uncalibrated centroid error prevent this flux estimate from excluding those stars. The observed location favors the Gaia target, but a calibrated crowded-field difference analysis remains the material missing check.
