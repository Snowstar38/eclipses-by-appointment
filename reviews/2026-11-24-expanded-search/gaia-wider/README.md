# Gaia orbital conjunctions and TESS eclipses

The second pass identified 96 additional stars in the same 15,981-source Gaia orbital sample. Selection required an orbital period below 65 days and a second-faintest measurement in a conjunction window at least 3.35 robust scatter units below the median. The conjunction score exceeded all three control-phase scores by more than two scatter units. The search also limited the strongest out-of-window dimming. Earlier TESS follow-ups and the complete 66-target first pass were removed. One candidate already appears in the atlas under another identifier.

The screening table covers all 96 stars. The detailed table reports the 11 with strong recurrence in at least two sectors and a fold prominence exceeding ten times the off-eclipse scatter. Direct VSX API requests began returning HTTP 405 during this pass. The CDS mirror of the AAVSO catalogue dated 18 October 2026 was checked using Algol as a positive control; the two proposed stars have their VSX variability classifications recorded. The official API returned a direct response for TIC 253926620. Each source JSON records the photometric and positional evidence, individual events and prior identifications. The image overlays independently folded sectors. A deep single minimum with no coherent second eclipse can also represent pulsation, as illustrated by the held QX Aqr case.

The period comes from a box-least-squares search within six reported uncertainties of the Gaia orbital period. TESS quality-zero simple or corrected aperture photometry was median-normalized on a two-day scale in contiguous segments and averaged into 20-minute bins. The table gives the search box width and stacked fractional depth in parts per thousand. The epoch is the flux-loss-weighted centre of an observed primary eclipse, in BJD TDB minus 2457000. Cadence limits the timing precision. An individual strong event exceeds both three local baseline MAD-based scatter units and 30 percent of the fitted depth. A separate raw SAP measurement interpolates a linear baseline between flanking windows and repeats the measurement a quarter of an orbit before and after each primary. Some short-period stars also vary away from eclipse.

Difference-image checks compare the free and target-constrained eclipse positions with the positions of capable Gaia sources. Each record has the sector, image origin and checksum when an image was retrieved. TESScut links in the reconstruction field request the equivalent sky region. Publicly accessible TESS light curves and pixel files can be retrieved by MAST URI. The stated conjunction offsets use the nearest of the two Gaia orbit predictions and the propagated orbit errors. The error ratios are approximate.

Catalogue comparisons include the AAVSO VSX API, VizieR variability and EB catalogues, Gaia DR3 Table Access Protocol, SIMBAD references and aliases, public ADS full-text alias matches, MAST DV files, saved TCE inventories, and live Villanova membership. The source records preserve publicly indexed detections including TCEs and candidate-only entries. Comparisons also cover the local atlas, released Chopard-Vilhem source and candidate tables, the SuperWASP false-positive sample, the 280-system spectroscopic compilation, the 3,425-row IJspeert et al. 2021 and 69,057-row IJspeert et al. 2024 EB catalogues, and Tang et al.'s 514 eccentric EBs. A negative result describes the searched records at the time of review.
