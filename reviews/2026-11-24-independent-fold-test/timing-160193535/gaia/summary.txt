Gaia DR3 SB2 clock versus TIC 160193535 deep TESS events

Result: The Gaia published 12.1326107107 +/- 0.0010372915 day period cannot place the observed deep events at one fixed phase or even two stable arbitrary conjunction phases. S19 alone has three deep, resolved minima at BTJD 1821.429324, 1829.929405, 1838.575272, corresponding to phases 0.04227, 0.74287, 0.45549. The smallest circular separation among these three is 0.2874 cycles (3.49 d), far greater than sampling and period uncertainty over this 17-day span. Any assignment of three events to two fixed phases must place at least two together. Thus this mismatch does not depend on assuming primary/secondary phases are half an orbit apart, nor on Gaia's epoch zero point. Shallowest-sampled minimum timing is not a formal fit, but cannot plausibly account for a multi-day discrepancy.

This makes the association positional, not proof that TESS aperture variability belongs to the Gaia SB2: large TESS pixels may blend other sources, and a hierarchical or chance-aligned system is possible. Nor does timing mismatch disprove the Gaia period for that source: aliases or incorrect spectroscopic fit are possible, especially given goodness of fit. Centroid/pixel-level checks and independent spectroscopy are needed to identify which source and orbit generate the eclipses.
