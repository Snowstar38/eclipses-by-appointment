# Independent helper audit

The helper read the current Section 2.7 definitions, delegation brief and privacy boundary. This audit concerns the lead's 2026-10-31 numeric freeze. `helper-regression.py` independently recomputes centered weighted slopes, uncertainties and epoch intercepts from the included actual event midpoints, with the same half-cadence/formal event uncertainties and scatter inflation; it records the measurement-file SHA-256 values in `helper-regression.json`. All ten agree within 1e-9 d in period and uncertainty and 1e-7 d in epoch. The two-event formal fit has zero residual degrees of freedom and its allowance is not a calibrated total uncertainty.

TIC 240786248: seven accepted real events including cycles 100,102,103,105,168,170,171 yield 10.7677990999 +/- 0.0000131461 d. Adjacent cycle recurrence checks the event interval; even and odd cycles are observed in different sectors, so sector-dependent dilution confounds a parity test and a doubled orbital alias cannot be excluded by depth equality alone. Gaia 10.76826 d supports the adopted interval.

TIC 377129604: visually inspected S72 contains two real deep eclipses. Former shallow S45/S46 fits are spurious and excluded; the lead examined earlier whole-sector raw flux. The two measured midpoints give 18.2332343366 +/- 0.0016369851 d separation and no period scatter estimate. Their depths differ ~50.8 and 60.6 ppt (custom aperture ~46.8 and 56.3 ppt); different eclipse kinds and doubled period remain possible. A plausible outcome and explicit association/period ambiguity are supported. Same-photon alternative reductions do not supply another epoch.

TIC 601646479: raw S57/S84 show systematic alternating eclipse depths; 13 deeper same-kind midpoints give 3.5358475506 +/- 0.00000326717 d. Custom aperture S57 gives ~21.7–23.2 ppt versus ~13.4–14.9 ppt alternating depths. The capable G=11.6351 neighbor 1.893 arcsec away is unresolved from the target by TESS; historical deficit centroid lies 0.137 px from neighbor and 0.165 px from target, without calibrated centroid errors. This supports real repeating eclipses but does not establish their source; probable attribution is appropriate.
