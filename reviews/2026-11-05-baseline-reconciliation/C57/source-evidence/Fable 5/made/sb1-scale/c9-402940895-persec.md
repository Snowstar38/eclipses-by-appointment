# TIC 402940895: per-object window-stack with empirical floor (2026-11-02)

The c9 held marginal (`c9-marg-persec.md`): eclipse or RS CVn spot-lock?
This is the 59955223 instrument (`c8-marg-persec.md`, `sec59.py`,
`refit59955223.py`), adapted. **The recorded trap is avoided**: there's no
quadratic detrend anywhere. Every window gets a local *linear* baseline

cross-check. Judged from numbers, not images.

## Verdict: PROMOTE as an eclipser (grazing/partial eclipse of the giant primary by the SB1's M-dwarf companion)

What kills spot-lock is not one number but three that agree:

1. **The dip is 12.8 σ_emp above the active baseline's own floor.** The
   stacked on-ephemeris depth is +6790 ppm over 5 windows. 161 off-ephemeris
   trial phases (0.10–0.90) give a null with median +47 ppm and robust sd
   527 ppm, and none of them reaches the on-phase stack (the largest is
   +2172). A scrambled-epoch null (each window redrawn from its own sector,
   20000 stacks) gives +12.9 σ_emp, 0/20000.
2. **The dip is fixed while the spot wave wanders.** The 7–18 ppt
   out-of-dip modulation fitted at P_orb puts its minimum at orbital phase
   0.84 / 0.84 / 0.33 / 0.20 (s12 / s39 / s66 / s93). The spots are *not*
   locked to the orbit. The dip meanwhile holds phase to rms 2.5 h
   (≤ 0.011 phase) over 135 cycles, 2019.40 → 2025.48.
3. **The width rules out a rotating spot.** The stacked FWHM is 0.40 d
   (9.6 h, 0.024 phase) with a flat bottom. A spot on a star rotating at
   ≥ 16 d cannot make a 10-h feature. A companion crossing a 3.33 R☉
   giant at a ≈ 32 R☉ can: the predicted central duration is 0.57–0.64 d,
   and the observed span is ~0.55 d.

The "no sharp edges" complaint was per-window noise: ~2 ppt rms per 30-min
bin against a 7 ppt dip. The stack has the edges, with ingress from
−0.30 to −0.17 d and egress from +0.17 to +0.35 d (profile below).

## (c) Phase drift

Per-window time of minimum (depth maximised over a ±0.4 d offset) and a
weighted linear fit over 5 minima spanning cycles 0–135:

- **P_fit = 16.43920 ± 0.00096 d**, T0 1632.1758, rms O−C 2.54 h,
  χ²/dof 5.4/3. A quadratic O−C term buys Δχ² 1.4, i.e. no drift.
- O−C (h): 2019.40 −2.4 · 2019.45 +1.7 · 2021.43 +1.3 · 2025.43 +1.4 ·
  2025.48 −4.5. That's scatter, not a wander: an evolving spot group would
  move by days over 6 yr, while this stays within ±0.011 phase.
- **P_fit vs Gaia: −1.84 σ** (Gaia 16.44814 ± 0.00486). Gaia's P error
  alone drifts a conjunction by 0.66 d (0.040 phase) over this span. The
  59955223 promotion was −1.9 σ.
- Conjunction attribution (`persec402_gaia.py`, Monte Carlo over Gaia's
  P, Tp, e, ω): the dips sit −0.099 / −0.089 / −0.114 / −0.161 / −0.177
  phase from Gaia's primary-eclipsed conjunction, against Gaia 1σ of
  0.083–0.101. That's z −1.1 to −1.75. The offset grows with time exactly
  as the −1.84σ P difference predicts, so the dip is at Gaia's
  conjunction within Gaia's precision (ω ± 20° dominates). Against the
  other conjunction it's +0.38 ± 0.14.

## Systematics check

At the 5 dips, the SAP_BKG core-minus-oot shift has inconsistent sign
(z +2.1, +1.2, +0.2, −1.1, −2.4), so the background doesn't drive the
dip. SAP_X/Y centroid shifts are −0.5 to −2.3 mpx, all within each
sector's empirical centroid floor (X percentile 8.6–37, z_emp −0.3 to
−2.7; Y percentile 21–63). The naive white-noise z of −5.4 in s39 X is
red noise. There's no significant centroid motion. X does lean the same
direction at all 5 dips, which is worth a look on the diffimg pass the
batch already owes. A blend isn't excluded by this pass; that's
diffimg's job. This pass only rules on eclipse vs spot/systematics.
