# Independent helper audit — batch 02

Scope: inspected the current Section 2.7 and 2.8 methods, all ten batch `raw-events.png` plots and `measurements.json` files, all ten historical `diff_sNN.png` and corresponding `diff_sNN.json` neighbour summaries in `Fable 5/made/sb1-scale/diffimg/<TIC>/`, and the batch `gaia-geometry.json`. Independently recomputed the weighted slopes from the stored usable midpoints and timing weights. This is an audit of saved measurements, not a separate extraction or all-sector search. Final RESULTS.csv and all ten reports were checked against the saved measurement values at their displayed precision: period, period error, epoch and depth agree. At the initial review I recommended seven confirmed and three probable, including TIC 368260545, because its centroid uncertainty was uncalibrated. See the dated follow-up below for the new evidence and final judgment.

All ten raw-event plots show repeated eclipse-shaped depressions. The raw examples include multiple sectors, with weaker events (especially TIC 21455449 and 409852500) noisier than the deep cases. Individual event fits and per-event cadence-limited errors remain model-dependent. The weighted slopes reproduced the ten stored periods to within 2.1e-13 d using stored usable midpoints; in 142427408 and 247204810 I used `orbital_cycle`, not half-period cycle. This verifies arithmetic only, not the uniqueness of cycle assignments or calibrated error coverage. For 142427408, measured ~86 ppt and ~41 ppt alternating events make the 5.0666841084 d same-kind period appropriate; singular midpoint fits are excluded. For 247204810 the same-kind fit is 9.5930932200 d; its raw examples demonstrate repeated dips, while the small depth differences make kind assignment less visually decisive than in 142427408. Gaia's 9.5940302 d SB1 period supports the doubled rather than half-period orbital interpretation. Epochs identify the selected event kind, not an independently established stellar identity.

Pixel evidence is conditional on its spatial resolution and background/PRF systematics. TIC 251731963 has Gaia G=12.26628 neighbour at 3.687 arcsec versus target G=12.03277; the required intrinsic neighbour eclipse is feasible (~27% in historical flux-budget estimate). The s81 difference-image center does not distinguish sources at this separation. TIC 409852500 has two capable G=16.32884 and 16.27829 neighbours at 7.31 and 9.46 arcsec, requiring ~41% and ~39% intrinsic eclipses in the saved estimate. The s13 deficit is close to the target (0.108 pixel), but those neighbours are only 0.35–0.45 pixel away; the saved centroid has no calibrated uncertainty and the PSF fractional residual is 0.817. The image alone does not robustly exclude either. Neighbour intrinsic eclipse depths are not comparable to the blended per-pixel fractional loss. TIC 368260545 has a capable G=12.299 neighbour 22.9 arcsec away; the s53 deficit lies closer to the target than that neighbour, but attribution remains unresolved because centroid uncertainty and PRF systematics are not calibrated. Other fields have more distant capable neighbours documented in the corresponding JSON; their image localization should be judged relative to those positions, not brightness alone.

The saved Gaia geometry compares measured photometric ephemerides against each SB1 period and edge-on conjunction with the orbit covariance and records approximate mass-function and duration plausibility. These are consistency checks, not proof of source attribution or fitted inclinations. Approximate central durations use assumed stellar properties. The alternative reductions use the same TESS photons; the historical TESScut difference images independently reduce pixel data and detect deficits, but are not independent observing occasions. No independent code re-extraction, catalogue novelty audit, or exhaustive search was performed by this helper. Flagged predicted windows cannot be counted as clean nulls; sparse real recurrence can test aliases without providing precision midpoints.

## Subsequent lead resolution (added by Sol; not a revised Terra finding)

The helper's 7/3 classification above was a cautious provisional assessment. Sol recomputed the four individual sector-53 deficit centroids for TIC 368260545 from the original TESScut FITS using the historical core/side windows (`368260545/pixel-event-audit.json`, `audit_368_pixels.py`). All four deficit centers lie closer to the target (0.13–0.47 pixel) than to the capable G=12.30 neighbor (0.75–1.07 pixel); the mean center is 0.20 versus 0.93 pixel from those positions. Together with the stacked peak on the target pixel, this event-specific evidence supports target attribution under the established bounded protocol. Sol therefore changed only TIC 368260545 to confirmed, yielding 8 confirmed and 2 probable. The neighbor and uncalibrated centroid limitation remain explicit in its report. Terra did not re-audit that additional extraction.


## 2026-11-01 follow-up: TIC 368260545

After my initial caution, the lead supplied a targeted per-event pixel audit. I inspected `audit_368_pixels.py` and independently recomputed the four event centroids from the actual sector 53 TESScut FITS and historical event-window centers, without writing the science artifact. All four recomputed centers match `pixel-event-audit.json` exactly; the in-event sample counts were 16, 16, 16 and 15, with 25–26 samples in each side window. Each centroid is 0.13–0.47 pixel from the target versus 0.75–1.07 pixel from the capable neighbor, whose target separation is 1.115 pixel. The reported stack and per-window results are consistent with the report. The four windows share the same detector orientation, analysis choices and possible systematic errors; their scatter is descriptive, not a calibrated positional interval.

On the bounded standard, the repeated per-window preference, stack localization and target-pixel peak are sufficient to support the lead's confirmed attribution, with the close capable neighbor and calibration limitation retained in the report. My earlier probable recommendation preceded this additional evidence; it was not an agreement with the later classification. The final report and RESULTS row agree on confirmed, and their displayed period, uncertainty, epoch and depth agree with `measurements.json`. The final batch outcome is eight confirmed and two probable.
