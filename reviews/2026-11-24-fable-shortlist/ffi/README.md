# Fable’s full-frame shortlist

The November 22 TESS-SPOC full-frame target table contained 97 entries in its lower candidate tier with measured light curves. A further 741 entries had only a pipeline detection summary. These 97 measured stars form the full sample here. Three already appeared in the project’s November 23 individual review, including one accepted star. Among the remaining 94, ten had a nearby published source with a matching period or harmonic, 25 advanced to two-sector pixel checks, and 59 remain at the initial photometry stage. Each row gives its actual screening outcome. A matching period elsewhere in a 15-arcmin field warrants checking for contamination and chance coincidence. It does not by itself identify the source. [Full 97-target table](measured-ffi-triage.csv)

The 25 received difference-image tests at the initial trial primary and secondary phases in two separated TESS sectors. TESS-SPOC pixel images and MIT QLP light curves are distinct reductions of the same spacecraft observations. Twenty apertures have a nearby Gaia star that could produce the initial measured depth. Two require further analysis of the orbital period and weaker dip. Three contain a known eclipse on a different star. These are screening outcomes at the original trial folding periods. Later work on a particular star can refine its cycle and classification. ### How to read the measurements

The initial period, time and depth columns in the 97-row table reproduce Fable’s light-curve fit. Its period can measure the spacing between alternating primary and secondary events. The original candidate-table period sometimes differs by a factor of two. Both values and the original note are preserved for every star in [original photometry](original-photometry/). Epochs are BJD TDB minus 2,457,000. A median QLP depth in the phase table uses individually detected events only; the per-source files retain measurements of all the covered events. A stack can have too little coverage or return a weak or displaced deficit. Those outcomes are explicit. The discovery-stage Gaia identifiers were rounded in Fable’s CSV export. The identifiers in the pixel table come from the unrounded Gaia scene data.

To select the 25, we first compared TICs with the project review tables. We compared positions and trial periods with the full IJspeert 2021 and 2024 eclipse tables and the Prša TESS catalogue. We screened exact TICs, positions within 30 arcseconds and matching periods within 15 arcminutes. The period tolerance was 0.2% at one-quarter, one-half, one, two and four times the trial period. The subsequent light-curve screen required at least three significant primary events, at least 40% of covered events detected, a primary depth of at least 4 parts per thousand, an initial secondary significance of at least five, a fractional width no greater than 0.14, a consistent blind period and detections in at least two pipeline sectors. The endpoint sectors for each shortlisted star were inspected at both minima. The [phase table](phase-measurements.csv) records the target fit, centroid offset, unresolved Gaia alternatives, product URLs and pixel-file hashes. [Source measurements](sources/)

Eleven catalogue queries with known positive controls checked positions within 30 arcseconds for the 97 input sources and the three suggested neighbours. The roster includes VSX, ASAS-SN, ZTF, ATLAS, two Gaia variability tables, three Kostov et al. tables, IJspeert and Prša. All eleven controls returned. Kostov Table 2 is an earlier machine list.
