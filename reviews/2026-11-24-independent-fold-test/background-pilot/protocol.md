# Background and eclipse pilot

The fresh cohort is 20 of 69 eligible two-minute apertures. The sampling frame comprises previously rejected pipeline folds with a paired-bin loss of at least 10 parts per thousand and an absolute ecliptic latitude at most 12 degrees. We excluded the earlier pilots, the published catalogue, Fable's full-frame batch and changing-system positions by exact TIC and within 30 arcseconds. We took the first 20 hashes salted with `bg-pilot-v1/`. We use the first and last eligible sector listed per TIC, for 37 sector light curves. The roster was posted before measuring the fresh light curves. This enriched sample measures feasibility in rejected apertures.

The unit of assessment is an event window. A known body's predicted path through a mask is geometric evidence. The light curves and pixels must establish how the recorded flux changed during the passage. An eclipse and a moving object can share a window. A failed catalogue service, incomplete exposure, or field containing a faint candidate remains a qualified result. Keep an unexplained loss available for stellar and other follow-up. The two previously identified crossings and HD 276904 supply development controls. Their outcomes are reported separately from the fresh cohort.

An additional paired stress test uses one fixed location on the time axis of the earliest available sector per fresh aperture. Choose the first adequately covered window at or after 32% of the elapsed range. Selection may use times, quality flags and the availability of a positive baseline. It cannot use the classifier's output. Compare the original flux, a simulated background overestimate, an eclipse alone, and both changes together on the same noisy measurements. Deposit a synthetic smooth sky overestimate with peak 20% of the local stellar flux into SAP_BKG and subtract it from SAP. Simulate stellar eclipse depths of 1.5%, 5% and 15% by subtracting light from SAP alone. Evaluate at the known injection time with the same photometric classification rules used on real events. This tests classification at a supplied time. A full survey also depends on event detection, spatial evidence and ephemeris coverage.
