## Why the physical tier changes

The original C57 review found baseline-sensitive depressions in sector 12 and corrupted sector 103 flux, with no secure eclipse sequence. Fable's later local-linear re-extraction measures five positive windows: two in sector 12, one in 39 and two in 93, at cycles 0, 1, 45, 134 and 135. The respective depths are 5.361, 6.850, 7.885, 6.570 and 7.286 ppt; their arithmetic mean is 6.790 ppt. The five minima span about six years and fit **P = 16.43920 d**, versus Gaia SB1 **16.448138 ± 0.004861 d**. Fable's weighted-fit formal period output is ±0.00096 d, but the per-event timing weights come from the half-maximum extent of a depth-versus-offset curve (`persec402.py` lines 296–299). Thus ±0.00096 d is a crude internal fit precision, **not a calibrated 1σ or prediction allowance**. The stated O−C rms is 2.54 h. This is repeat evidence, not a one-event fold. Sector 66's predicted center falls in a data gap; sectors 101–103 have 72–121 ppt point-to-point scatter versus about 1.95 ppt in the usable sectors and were excluded by a stated >5× median rule. These are unavailable checks, not null eclipses.

The on-phase mean depth exceeds an off-phase phase-scan median by 6.743 ppt with 0.527-ppt robust scatter, or 12.8 empirical scatter units; no off-phase stack of 161 reached it. A separate 20,000-draw scrambled-epoch check also had none as deep. These are tests against the tested phase/epoch nulls, not a calibrated survey false-alarm probability. Drop-one-sector stacks remain at 10.1–12.5 empirical scatter units. The stacked core is about 0.40 d FWHM, while the broad spot modulation changes phase across sectors. A spot-only explanation is disfavored. The measured dip is compatible with, though does not prove, a grazing transit by the Gaia SB1's faint companion across the giant primary. The Gaia conjunction agreement is approximate. Fable's Monte Carlo drew P, Tp, e and ω as **independent** Gaussians, without the published covariance, so its −1.1 to −1.75 standard-deviation offsets are descriptive, not calibrated multivariate significance. The measured period difference alone is −1.84 Gaia period standard deviations.

Fable separately extracted TESS FFI cutouts in sectors 12, 39 and 93. Each **stacked** deficit image peaks on the target pixel, with aperture depths 7.14, 6.36 and 7.57 ppt in 3×3 boxes and off-phase empirical significance 14.1, 7.9 and 10.2. The sector offsets from target are 4.38, 10.77 and 1.94 arcsec; their empirical mean offset is 1.72 arcsec, with between-sector standard errors 3.07 arcsec east and 3.50 arcsec north. This extraction checks source morphology independently of QLP's light-curve reduction but uses the **same TESS photons**, not an independent observation. In sector 39, both individual deficits lean northwest; one single-event peak is off-target. Two Gaia sources 10.37 and 10.43 arcsec away in that direction are flux-capable if they eclipse intrinsically by roughly 42% or 67%. Those percentages are Gaia-G flux-ratio estimates assuming target-dominated aperture light, not precise TESS-band dilution requirements. The three-sector centroid evidence favors the target but **does not exclude this pair**. Wider neighbors (≥26 arcsec) are disfavored by the images and centroid distances.

The paper's status definition requires a confirmed system to pass a difference-image attribution against *every* neighbor bright enough to produce the deficit. Because the close pair remains capable and unresolved, **PROBABLE** is the conservative physical tier: repeated real eclipses, one attribution check incomplete. The next decisive check is PRF fitting of the three sector cutouts, or higher-resolution time-series photometry at a predicted eclipse. An exact ephemeris suitable for observer-facing predictions needs a calibrated timing-error/alias check; the ±0.00096 d output should not be propagated as a prediction allowance.
