# Methods and data dictionary

The survey examined repeated light losses in TESS photometry from Gaia orbit searches and radial-velocity selections. It compared individual visits, raw aperture flux and detrended flux, event periods and phase controls. Difference images test whether the fading light originates at the catalogued target. An alternative TESS extraction checks sensitivity to aperture or pipeline choices. Separate visits and ground-based photometry provide physically independent samples. When a relevant Gaia orbit is available, its conjunction timing and uncertainty are compared with the eclipse.

**Confirmed** systems pass the relevant tests that their observations support. **Probable** systems have recurring losses with a material remaining check, such as sparse independent photometry, blending, shallow signal or uncertain geometry. A confirmed eclipse can still have a conditional orbital period. An observed primary recurrence records the repeated minima, and period interpretations identify cases where the analysis resolves the orbital clock.

The historical check compared eleven catalogue tables within 30 arcseconds and seven partly overlapping tables within 90 arcseconds, alongside identifier-based SIMBAD, ADS and Villanova searches and checks of relevant primary survey tables. The matches and completeness flags apply to the sources and query scopes used. An exact machine-selected candidate has prior search evidence, and the physical results here can validate that earlier flag. A nearby match is kept distinct from the target unless its clock and source position are consistent with the observed loss. [TESS Eclipsing Binary Catalog](https://tessebs.villanova.edu/) · [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cfa.harvard.edu/simbad/) · [NASA ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/).

## Tables and measurements

- A **TIC** or **Gaia DR3 source ID** is an identifier. Import those fields as text so spreadsheet software preserves every digit. Positions are in decimal degrees on ICRS.
- Times labeled **BTJD** are BJD on the TDB time scale minus 2,457,000. Reference epochs and periods retain numerical working precision. Future eclipse midpoints can have additional timing errors. An empty ephemeris or measurement means the source table does not supply one.
- `host-stars.csv` gives one row per attributed system. `field-outcomes.csv` gives one row per selected aperture. `evidence_file` and `evidence_key` identify the corresponding JSON entry under `targets`. Identifiers on a field and its inferred source can differ. Review IDs are stable across the two tables where the selected source enters the host table.
- JSON measurement families include `tess_visit_measurements` for radial-velocity cases and `photometry` plus `tess_event_windows` for many other cases. Photometric fields ending in `_ppt` are parts per thousand, and `raw_median`, `det_median` and most unitless depth fields are flux fractions. Positive loss is dimming. Ground-survey `mag` and `delta_mag` values are magnitude differences.
- A **shifted control** evaluates similar windows away from the tested phase. The number of shifts as deep is a diagnostic of repeatability, correlated noise and other features in the folded light curve. It is distinct from a survey-wide false-alarm probability. The individual photometry records preserve the available number of tested shifts.
- **Localization** records loss-image centroid offsets in pixels or arcseconds, as indicated by field names. One TESS pixel spans about 21 arcseconds. For nearby possible contaminants, a flux-budget calculation tests whether the star could supply the lost light. An apparently centered signal can still include unresolved sources.
- `archive_products` lists available public MAST identifiers with file hashes, sector and cadence information. `archive_search` supplies target and sector identifiers when no original product identifier was retained in the measurements. A MAST identifier beginning with `mast:` can be passed to the [MAST download service](https://mast.stsci.edu/portal/Mashup/Clients/Mast/Portal.html). Alternative reductions may use the same photons. The tables preserve measured indicators and source summaries at the available coverage. FITS pixels and raw survey light curves remain at the public archives.

The diagnostic examples contain event CSVs, small derived pixel arrays, observing windows, and source notes. Run `python examples/plot-examples.py` with Python 3, NumPy and Matplotlib to generate reduced plots from the released measurements.
