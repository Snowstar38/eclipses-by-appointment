# Batch 02 lead audit — frozen queue positions 11–20

All ten exact TICs in `queue.csv` received a report and result row. No replacements. The reviewed sample has actual Gaia DR3 G 12.021002–12.034502. Eight physical verdicts are confirmed; TIC 251731963 and 409852500 are probable because capable close neighbors remain unresolved in TESS pixels. All ten bounded reviews are complete. The central seven-group catalogue and full Gaia NSS audit reports complete coverage and no identified prior EB for these exact ten; catalogue status remains separate from physical verdict.

Raw QLP SAP flux was downloaded from the frozen target QLP inventory (31 FITS across these ten; `download-receipts.json` and `source-receipts.json` hold URI, identity, hash and byte receipts). Representative measured eclipse centers and local raw flux are in each `measurements.json` and `raw-windows.csv`; all ten show repeated real events. The QLP sectors include the historical pixel-stack sector for each target. TIC 142427408 has strongly alternating event depths, and TIC 247204810 has modest alternation: both timing fits use the odd historical half-period parity, yielding orbital periods 5.066684108 and 9.593093220 d. Stored event centers, cycle indices and errors permit direct reproduction. Period errors are covariance errors using max(formal trapezoid timing error, half cadence), inflated for excess scatter, and do not claim calibrated total coverage. Primary epoch fields name the selected eclipse kind in each report.

TIC 409852500 sector-104 cycle-294 was retained as an eclipse morphology example but excluded from timing because a 0.10417-d gap truncates egress at about +0.12 to +0.22 d from its fitted center. Five other usable centers give P=8.643951250 d. No target is an apparent single-cycle case in the inspected raw sectors, so the contingent current-sector availability check was not triggered. Gaia edge-on conjunctions, covariance, SB1 mass functions and approximate central-duration plausibility were computed per row in `gaia-geometry.json`. The conjunction offsets are small, within about 0.031 phase; these are physical consistency checks rather than source-localization proofs.

Historical TESScut stacks and Gaia neighbor lists were read for every target, with all Gaia neighbors within 60 arcsec tested for flux capability. The alternative aperture depths and difference images re-reduce the same TESS photons. `pixel-evidence.json` in each TIC folder records source files, offsets, aperture depths and every capable neighbor. Intrinsic neighbor-required eclipse depths were not compared directly with blended pixel fractional losses.

Three attribution cases merited specific attention. TIC 251731963 has a nearly equally bright Gaia star 3.69 arcsec away; a 27% intrinsic eclipse there is feasible, and the 0.024-pixel target offset cannot resolve the pair. TIC 409852500 has two G≈16.3 neighbors at 7.31 and 9.46 arcsec requiring ≈40% eclipses; the target-favoring 0.108-pixel centroid lacks the resolution and calibrated spatial uncertainty to exclude them. Both remain probable. TIC 368260545 has a similarly bright capable neighbor 22.87 arcsec away. Its original four-window stacked deficit was 0.206 pixel from the target versus 0.92 pixel from that neighbor, with peak deficit on the target pixel. In a targeted recomputation from the original s53 cutout, **all four individual windows** localized closer to target (0.13–0.47 pixel) than neighbor (0.75–1.07 pixel); mean distance 0.20 versus 0.93 pixel. This event-specific localization supports confirmed under the established bounded standard, while the uncalibrated centroid limitation is retained. `audit_368_pixels.py` and `368260545/pixel-event-audit.json` are the deciding additional artifacts.
