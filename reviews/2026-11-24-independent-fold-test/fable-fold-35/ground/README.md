# Ground photometry at the TESS predictions

We queried ZTF g and r measurements within 2.16 arcseconds of each of the 35 target positions. The archive returned measurements at 31 positions and empty results at four. The period, epoch and eclipse width were supplied by TESS throughout. For every target we tested Sol's preferred box-search solution, including its low-confidence trials. We also tested the 13 alternate clocks supplied by Fable. For those 13 we used the median measured core width of Fable's selected TESS dips within 0.12 day of a predicted primary. We did not fit ground observations to improve an ephemeris. [Query receipts and raw-response hashes](receipts.json) · [Fable's TESS-only inputs](fable-clocks.csv) · [ZTF archive API](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/program_interface/ztf_lightcurve_api.html)

For each ZTF object and filter, we retained quality-zero detections with magnitude errors below 0.2 mag and at least 30 good measurements. We converted mid-exposure UTC to barycentric TDB from Palomar. The eclipse core extends 0.36 of the supplied full width to either side, capped at 4% of a cycle on each side. We subtracted the median magnitude for the object and filter, computed the median dimming in each sampled cycle, then took the median over cycles.

A positive check requires at least four distinct cycles in the core and dimming of at least three times the larger of the full-series robust scatter, the median reported uncertainty and 0.005 mag. We shifted the prediction around the orbit at 100 evenly spaced offsets and compared those with at least three covered cycles outside twice the core half-width. At least 40 usable offsets and 95% shallower offsets were required. Offsets can overlap and multiple objects, bands and trial periods were tested. These diagnostics do not give a trials-corrected false-positive probability. Sparse sampling can miss an eclipse. [All object/filter/clock combinations with usable photometry](phase-checks.csv) · [Rendered light curves](recoveries.png)

Run `python fetch.py CACHE`, `python test_clock.py CACHE` and `python plot.py CACHE` here. The figure uses the successful trial with the most sampled cycles per star and plots the available good g and r photometry at that prediction. Consult the per-object table for the bands that passed. Requirements are Requests, pandas, NumPy, Astropy and Matplotlib.
