# TIC 187261476 sector 59 pixel check

Both independently imaged signals are spatially consistent with the bright target, and with each other at this resolution. This supports a common on-target origin; it does not prove a common physical system or exclude unresolved contaminants. SPOC calibrated target-pixel and light-curve products were retrieved from the URLs in fetch.py; FITS SHA-256s, windows and numeric fits are in evidence.json. The plot difference.png marks the TIC coordinate with a red cross and Gaia DR3 sources as white crosses labelled by G magnitude.

The recurring 17 dips were independently identified in the unfolded PDCSAP series; the entire deep-event interval was excluded. For each, the per-pixel difference is the average of local left/right median baseline images minus the median in-event image; the 17 difference images are median-combined. The separate deep event uses BTJD 2917.54-2917.93 and the adjacent 2917.00-2917.25 and 2918.25-2918.50 baselines. All difference-image cadences have QUALITY=0. Data are calibrated FLUX pixels; no further background subtraction or PSF calibration is applied.

A simple descriptive symmetric Gaussian fit gives shallow (x,y)=(5.723,5.898), deep (5.741,5.934), and target WCS position (5.639,5.845) in zero-based cutout pixels. Shallow/deep difference is 0.040 pixel; their offsets from target are 0.099 and 0.135 pixel. These are approximate Gaussian centroids, not empirical TESS PRF fits; no error bars or formal association significance are inferred. Pixel response, WCS and event-baseline systematics can shift positions. The image morphology itself peaks on the same target pixels for both events.

The closest Gaia DR3 source, 200346136869087616 (G=9.967), lies 0.318 arcsec from the given TIC coordinate at its unpropagated catalog epoch. The nearest other Gaia source within the checked G<19 cone, 200346141165207936, is 5.45 arcsec away and G=15.96, about 5.99 mag fainter (roughly 1/250 of target G-band flux). That source alone is far too faint to account for a ~400 ppt target-aperture drop even if fully extinguished, subject to passband/aperture calibration; its G brightness also makes it an unlikely source of the ~30 ppt repeated events. Gaia incompleteness, unresolved companions, and non-Gaia or different-color contaminants remain possible.

The pipeline flags cannot guarantee astrophysical origin; the deep event is a complex overlapping structure, and nearby shallow events plus baseline modulation may contaminate its difference image. A calibrated PRF fit with uncertainties, pixel time-series checks across independent sectors, high-resolution imaging or spatially resolved spectroscopy would strengthen source attribution.
