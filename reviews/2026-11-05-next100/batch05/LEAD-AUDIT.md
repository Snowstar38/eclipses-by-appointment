# Batch05 lead audit, Q041-Q050

This is a bounded physical review of the sealed ten-target batch. `lead_context.py` verifies every cached QLP FITS SHA256 against the packet, reads quality-zero positive SAP, plots whole-sector context and saved local time series. `lead_flag_diagnostics.py` measures actual flag core endpoints and nearby gaps. The lead separately inspected the raw plots; no screening score or catalogue label was converted to a physical verdict. `lead-neighbors.py` fetched a fresh hashed Gaia DR3 cone for every target. `lead-pixels.py` fetched six TESSCut WCS cutouts, retaining ZIP hashes and difference maps. Terra's independent audit is in `helper-audit.txt` and `helper-*` artifacts. Catalogue novelty is UNKNOWN for all ten because the four positive-control gates failed; see `../shared-catalogues/REPORT.md` and `TARGET-STATUS.csv`.

Q043 and Q044 are the deciding eclipse cases. For Q043, local Gaussian diagnostics on five clear raw SAP events (S18, S25, S58, unflagged S78, S79) give BTJD 1811.25859, 1990.69408, 2887.85657, 3448.58120, 3471.01097. Their integer cycles 0, 8, 48, 73, 74 give conditional P=22.429066 d, epoch 1811.260201, timing residuals within 0.0017 d. The S78 odd cycle establishes the 22.429 d photometric recurrence; two similar eclipse kinds in a 44.858 d orbit are not excluded by photometry alone, though the source-specific Gaia SB1 period supports 22.4 d. S59 n50 is noisy and not used in the timing fit. Nine actually covered P/2 windows are flat, as are representative P/3 and P/4 windows; `lead-alias-nulls.json` and `helper-null-coverage.json` retain cadence counts, endpoints, sidebands, and local depths. The independently fetched S18/S58 WCS difference centroids are 0.041/0.126 pixel from the Gaia target, with valid positive target flux, and 3x3 loss of 8.01/6.96 ppt. A capable Gaia G13.548 neighbor at 39.022 arcsec (flux ratio 0.1273) exists; the spatial difference peaks on the target in both cutouts. The S78 pixel comparison gave a pathological -412 ppt because its broad side windows are invalid; it is not used as a null or a source localization. Gaia's SB1 period 22.421921 ±0.010315 d agrees with the photometric period; using the photometric period with this target's eccentricity, omega, and periastron, the observed event differs from the calculated B conjunction by about 0.010 d. The orbital geometry is supporting context, not a substitute for measured timing. Verdict: CONFIRMED eclipse association, novelty UNKNOWN.

For Q044, the raw SAP shows recurring asymmetric troughs at S7/8, S61/62, S88/89; S89 contains two separate events at BTJD 3719.777 and 3743.172, about 23.395 d apart. The conditional multievent fit gives 23.400623 d, with broad/morphology-limited S61/S62 centers and S34 excluded as a sector trend. QLP SAP and independent TESSCut 3x3 curves reproduce the same two S89 structures: a rapid ingress near phase -0.35 d, a roughly flat core from -0.18 to +0.17 d, and rapid egress near +0.3 to +0.4 d. `lead-shape.json` measures 151-152 in-core cadences for each event and shows pre-event to core drops of 3.9/7.5 ppt in the alternate aperture. The larger smooth sector-wide rise and fall continues outside those contacts, so a broad orbital/tidal wave may coexist; the repeatable contact-like notch is the eclipse evidence. The repeated S61 and S89 WCS differences center within 0.134/0.091 pixel of the Gaia target, with positive valid target pixels and 3x3 loss about 7.04/9.75 ppt. Multi-era localization, covered flat P/2, P/3, P/4 aliases, and Gaia SB1 23.417146 ±0.011908 d support the association. With the measured photometric period, the target-specific A conjunction differs from the S8 diagnostic center by about 0.16 d, within broad event and Gaia periastron uncertainty. S88's broad-window pixel reduction is invalid as an independent confirmation; it is not counted as a null. Verdict: CONFIRMED; the Gaussian centers and quoted period are diagnostic, without formal precision error.

Q041's S94 apparent deep trough has a WCS difference dipole: target-centered 3x3 loss 5.58 ppt falls to 0.30 ppt over 9x9, with adjacent positive and negative pixels. Q049 has an almost simultaneous S94 trough at BTJD 3874.880 versus Q041 3874.873. Q041 S67 and S101 shallow events are 37 Gaia cycles apart but fresh pixel cutouts show dipoles and loss falling from 1.83 to 0.49 ppt and 0.66 to 0.30 ppt as aperture grows from 3x3 to 9x9. These are positive motion/redistribution evidence for the selected flags. A sub-ppt intrinsic component cannot be excluded. Gaia's expected opposite-conjunction phase separation (0.590/0.410) also fails to explain the deep S94 flag's ~0.706 phase from the two shallow ones, but geometry alone is not the refutation. Verdict: REFUTED selected eclipse signal, not a global binary exclusion.

Q042 has 41 raw sectors with repeated smooth rapid peaks and troughs and no discrete, stable eclipse morphology at its 89 dip flags. The 1.58-day-spaced detector windows sample the wave; the Gaia NSS period is 51.57 d. Q045, Q048, and Q049 flags are local slopes, discontinuities, or gap boundaries. Q045 S74 is a step between two levels; Q048 S56 is a flat-to-rising transition and S83 a long decline; Q049 S94 is gap/step affected, S101 a decline across a gap, S102/103 likewise lack a full occultation. These are REFUTED as selected target eclipse evidence. Other binary or variable behavior is not excluded.

Q046 has a target-centered S65 trough (TESSCut 3x3 loss ~15 ppt) and other broad irregular SAP features in S12/S38. Their timings do not recur as one kind at the Gaia 34.761 d SB1 clock, and KSPSAP suppresses much of the earlier broad structure. A single on-target eclipse-like event remains possible. Fresh TESSCut coverage includes S103, absent from the QLP cache, but its nominal target aperture has near-zero median sky-subtracted flux (0.118 count) and only 102 positive quality-zero values; it cannot supply a sensitive null. Verdict: PLAUSIBLE, period unmeasured.

Q047 S12 has isolated single-cadence ~5% lows; S93 has a long low SAP level bounded by gaps and a nearby capable Gaia G12.90 source only 12.4 arcsec away. A valid, positive-flux TESSCut 3x3 extraction centered on the target loses only ~1.6 ppt in S93 versus the ~27 ppt QLP SAP low section. The discrepancy and 0.54–0.75 pixel ambiguous difference centroid leave source ownership and even the cause of the SAP low unresolved. An off-target source is a follow-up hypothesis, not an identification. Fresh S104 TESSCut target aperture is near-zero flux (median ~0.427 count after positive filtering), so it is not a null. Verdict: PLAUSIBLE one eclipse-like occasion, with no adopted depth or period.

Q050's S88 four dip flags at 3694.967, 3696.425, 3697.939, 3699.495 are successive ~1.5-day troughs of a smooth wave, also seen in cached S7/S34/S61 and fresh valid S109 TESSCut 3x3 photometry. This defeats the claimed sparse 57.216-day eclipse recurrence. The S88 WCS difference near the target has a capable unresolved neighbor at 6.5 arcsec; source ownership of the wave is not asserted. S109 is a new valid sector (8122 positive quality-zero target-aperture points, median flux ~4157 count), with the same rapid oscillation. Verdict: REFUTED as selected long-period eclipse evidence; short-period variability remains a follow-up lead.
