# Independent helper audit — batch01

Scope: exact V001–V010 queue, with deciding follow-up requested by lead. The lead owns final classifications. No allocation or pilot ranking was read. Catalogue novelty is independent and remains pending the centralized gate results.

- **V007 / TIC 147413927:** Seven recurring SAP troughs and their cross-sector clock are persuasive. I inspected S37 and S90 raw, pixel differences and the new target-centered aperture curves. Both show smooth, roughly 4–5 ppt troughs with a pre-trough bright hump, without a clean distinct ingress/egress. Although eclipse plus heartbeat modulation is possible, heartbeat modulation alone remains a plausible interpretation. My independent judgment is **PLAUSIBLE**: real repeating eclipses are not yet established, and heartbeat-only remains materially viable. The recurring source signal is strong; a demonstrable eclipse profile or independent physical constraint is needed to promote the eclipse interpretation. S37 KSPSAP suppresses the shape; this is a reason to describe the discrepancy, not by itself proof of artifact. Two TESSCut reductions reproduce the deficits; these reuse the same photons. Core endpoints and gaps in `helper-aperture.json` show nearly complete coverage at native cadence; corner sky and target flux are finite positive. Difference peaks lie on the target pixel in two orientations (centroids 0.060 and 0.200 px from WCS target). A Gaia G16.85 star 14.99 arcsec away has approximate G flux ratio 0.00272: even its total disappearance at full throughput is below the ~4.4–4.5 ppt TESSCut deficits in the G approximation; the TESS bandpass differs. More distant capable stars are listed in `helper-capable-neighbors.json`. The Gaia SB1 geometry/period calculation belongs to the lead; agreement supports association but does not establish eclipse morphology.
- **V002 / TIC 265066551:** S17 high-minus-low difference signal is centered close to the queued coordinate, but a Gaia G8.1194 star only 8.706 arcsec away is ~18.15 times as bright in G as the G11.26689 target. Difference centroid is 0.275 px from the bright neighbor and 0.375 px from target; the two are unresolved at TESS scale. The strong 1.26-day variable cannot be uniquely assigned to the target from these pixels. See `helper-pixels.json`, cone receipt, and `helper-capable-neighbors.json`; G ratios are approximate and do not establish source identification.
- **V001/V008/V009:** Visually inspected lead's raw S94 comparison: independent widely separated stars show the same fine structure in a broad decline, followed by a gap and recovery. V001/V009 also show a concurrent S96 decline/gap. This is positive common-mode evidence against those particular selected dips as isolated stellar eclipses; it does not exclude unrelated eclipses elsewhere. No fresh pixel attribution was needed to establish this cross-target concurrence.
- **V004:** S99 4053.24 and S101 4107.72 show abrupt post-gap SAP steps/recovery accompanied by centroid displacement and relaxation, positive artifact evidence for these flags, not evidence the star cannot be binary.
- Remaining V003, V005, V006, V010: lead retains full review and classification; this helper did not independently re-extract them. No independent alias-null certification is asserted here.

## Reproducibility and limits

`helper-fetch.py` records downloaded TESSCut FITS hashes and URLs in `helper-fetch.json`; `helper-pixels.py`, `helper-aperture.py`, `helper-gaia-cone.py` and `helper-neighbors.py` generate the named helper receipts and plots. These are bounded checks, not a full PRF fit; faint catalog incompleteness, Gaia-to-TESS color differences and unresolved neighbors limit attribution. Helper source files were separately hashed in the corresponding receipts. Lead must freeze the entire batch manifest after all writes and independently verify it.
